# G8.4.8.9 — Counterfactual policy world models for adaptive agent security

**Status:** research specification and benchmark protocol; not implemented or empirically validated.  
**Parent context:** G8.4.8.7.7 decision-time predictive evaluation and G8.4.8.8 randomized intervention-utility study.  
**Research question:** can a policy-aware counterfactual world model estimate, for one observed agent episode, how alternative tool actions or restrictive orchestration interventions would have changed the subsequent security/task trajectory under the same exogenous environment realization?

**Scope boundary:** this is a new counterfactual-prediction and action-comparison study. It does not replace the G8.4.8.8 randomized strategy-level intervention trial, does not grant authority to learned models, and does not establish real-world causal effects from simulator-only counterfactuals.

## 1. Motivation and relation to the source method

Balestriero, LeCun, and Varaku (2026), *Counterfactual World Models: JEPAs That Reason About "What If"*, arXiv:2611.24778, studies an individual counterfactual: after observing the outcome of an executed action, predict the outcome that the same event would have produced under an alternative action, holding the realized disturbance fixed.

For representation \(z_t\), executed action \(a_t\), observed next representation \(z_{t+1}\), and factual predictor \(\mu(z_t,a)\), its residual-reuse construction is

\[
\widehat z_{t+1}(a')
=
z_{t+1}+\mu(z_t,a')-\mu(z_t,a_t).
\tag{1}
\]

The factual residual is retained and the model's predicted action contrast is transported to the alternative action. The source paper establishes exactness in additive response models, derives error in terms of action–disturbance interaction, and identifies failure cases including command-scaled disturbances, discontinuities, and folded representations. Its guarantees are conditional on structural assumptions; factual prediction quality alone does not identify an individual counterfactual.

This project must not re-label the source method as a new UCON-JEPA invention. The research opportunity is to determine whether, where, and how residual counterfactual transport can be used in **mixed discrete/continuous, policy-conditioned, adaptive-agent trajectories**, where authorization boundaries and agent replanning create discontinuities and treatment-dependent future histories.

The G8.4.8.8 intervention trial asks a different question: the ITT effect of assigning an entire intervention strategy. G8.4.8.9 asks whether a model predicts individual alternative-action outcomes for a fixed episode and whether those predictions rank candidate actions usefully. Neither estimand substitutes for the other.

## 2. Research objectives and falsifiable claims

| ID | Claim or hypothesis | Initial status |

| C8.9-1 | Individual counterfactual outcomes are not identified from factual transition distributions alone without additional coupling/structural assumptions. | THEORETICAL BASELINE |
| C8.9-2 | Residual reuse is exact for continuous readout outcomes under the additive shared-disturbance model in §5. | PROVABLE under stated premises |
| C8.9-3 | In the UCON simulator, residual reuse is reliable only in empirically qualified state/action regimes; global use across policy boundaries is not assumed. | OPEN / EMPIRICAL |
| H8.9-1 | Residual-transport CWM improves individual continuous-outcome counterfactual prediction over direct action-conditioned factual prediction in qualified smooth regimes. | OPEN / EMPIRICAL |
| H8.9-2 | A regime-aware hybrid (residual transport for qualified continuous outcomes, explicit structural/oracle evaluation for authorization labels, and direct prediction elsewhere) improves counterfactual prediction and action selection over either component alone. | OPEN / EMPIRICAL |
| H8.9-3 | A support-aware model that abstains on unsupported or structurally invalid action queries has lower selective risk than an always-answer model at matched coverage. | OPEN / EMPIRICAL |

## 3. Causal target and unit of analysis

Let \(H_t\) denote the complete permitted decision-time history and policy context for one episode. Let \(A_t\) be the agent's submitted action, \(E_t\) the exogenous disturbance realization, and \(Y_{t+1}\) the next outcome. The structural transition is

\[
Y_{t+1}=F(H_t,A_t,E_t).
\]

For alternative action \(a'\), the individual one-step counterfactual is

\[
Y_{t+1}(a';e_t)=F(H_t,a',e_t),
\tag{2}
\]

where the **same** \(H_t\) and same realized \(e_t\) are held fixed. This is not the interventional population mean \(\mathbb E[Y_{t+1}\mid do(A_t=a'),H_t]\), and not merely a plausible rollout conditioned on \(H_t,a'\).

For a multi-step counterfactual, define an alternative policy \(\pi'\) and reuse a declared exogenous tape \(\mathbf E_{t:t+H-1}\):

\[
\mathbf Y_{t+1:t+H}(\pi';\mathbf e)
=
\operatorname{Rollout}(H_t,\pi',\mathbf e).
\tag{3}
\]

After the first changed action, the state/history and agent's subsequent decisions are allowed to evolve under the alternative branch. They are not held equal to the factual future. The exogenous random stream is coupled by a predeclared common-random-number rule, not by reusing mutable PRNG state whose consumption order changes across branches.

### 3.1 Two distinct counterfactual questions

1. **Action substitution:** replace the submitted operation at decision \(t\) by a supported alternative \(a'\), while keeping the current context and exogenous realization fixed. Subsequent behavior follows the same frozen agent response policy under the resulting branch state.
2. **Orchestration intervention:** change the available-capability set or apply the fixed temporary overlay, then let the agent replan under the declared response contract. This is a dynamic intervention on the action-generation mechanism, not just a different tool-call label.

These queries must be evaluated separately. The second is directly relevant to G8.4.8.8, but its multi-step outcomes are not identified by a one-step action-substitution model without additional sequential assumptions.

## 4. Mixed outcome representation: do not add categorical labels

UCON trajectories contain both numerical/context variables and discrete policy events. A single vector-space residual operation over every field is invalid.

Partition outcomes into:
- \(Y^{(c)}\): continuous or numerically meaningful state quantities, such as risk-context scores, resource-use counters, accumulated cost, continuous workload/latency, and selected numeric state variables;
- \(Y^{(d)}\): discrete/categorical events, including UCON PERMIT/DENY, role-grant state, approval state, capability availability, task completion, revocation, and policy-version transitions;
- \(Y^{(e)}\): event sequences and structured traces, which require sequence/event metrics rather than Euclidean subtraction.

The counterfactual model is a **typed hybrid**, not an untyped latent-vector correction:

\[
\widehat Y(a')=
\left(
\widehat Y^{(c)}_{\mathrm{cf}}(a'),
\widehat P_\psi(Y^{(d)}\mid H_t,a'),
\widehat{\mathcal T}_\omega(a')
\right).
\tag{4}
\]

- Residual reuse may be applied only to predeclared continuous readouts whose representation and regime pass the qualification gates.
- Discrete UCON decisions are recomputed by the exact policy oracle from the branch's contemporaneous state and request. They are not predicted by adding residuals to numeric encodings of labels.
- Learned discrete heads may forecast future event probabilities for planning/evaluation, but their outputs never substitute for the authoritative UCON decision.
- Event traces are scored with exact event agreement, sequence edit distance, time-to-event error, and proper scoring rules.

A model must not claim an individual counterfactual DENY label merely because a continuous latent correction is close in Euclidean distance.

### 4.1 A genuine continuous security-context process is required

The current G8.4 UCON trajectory state is primarily categorical/Boolean (role granted, read completed, request seen, approved, violation) plus integer action-history counts. Casting these values to float does not make them continuous physical readouts. In particular, a counter or binary policy flag must not be used to manufacture a residual-CWM positive result.

The benchmark must therefore first audit which existing fields are genuinely continuous. If none satisfy the readout and regularity requirements, add a separately versioned **continuous security-context subsystem** to the synthetic environment. Candidate interpretable quantities include cumulative egress exposure, API/resource pressure, session anomaly intensity, or workload/latency. Their units, bounds, update semantics, and relation to task intent must be explicit; they are synthetic benchmark variables, not claims about real enterprise distributions.

Use two controlled structural mechanisms over the same state/action support:

**Positive-control separate-channel mechanism**
[
x_{t+1}=x_t+h,v(x_t,a_t)+h,w(x_t,arepsilon_t)+R_h,
qquad |R_h|_{L^2}le Ch^2.
]
The action and environmental disturbance enter as separate first-order terms. With exact conditional means, residual reuse should satisfy the protocol's conditional short-time prediction. The simulator-only paired check must verify the actual remainder scaling rather than assume it.

**Negative-control action-scaled mechanism**
[
x_{t+1}=x_t+h,v(x_t,a_t)
+h,lambda,v(x_t,a_t)arepsilon_t
+h,w(x_t,arepsilon_t)+R_h.
]
For (lambda
e0), the action contrast depends on the realized disturbance at first order. Residual reuse should lose its additive exactness; the measured action-effect variance must expose this interaction.

The UCON oracle may then use a separately declared threshold on a continuous context variable (for example, deny a sensitive export above an exposure threshold). This deliberately creates a **hybrid discontinuity**: residual reuse may be qualified for the continuous context coordinate while the categorical PERMIT/DENY outcome is recomputed exactly from the branch state. Test queries immediately below, at, and above the threshold. Never residual-correct the categorical verdict.

Factual training actions must have support. Use a declared exploration policy with independent randomization and positive probability for every queried action in each eligible stratum, or explicitly narrow the query bank to verified support. The randomization probability and action support are frozen before test generation. This action exploration is part of the synthetic data-collection design, not an intervention outcome.

If the continuous subsystem cannot be implemented with transparent units, stable dynamics, separate positive/negative mechanisms, and independent exogenous noise, the CWM experiment must remain a mathematical benchmark only; do not claim it models enterprise security context.

## 5. Formal residual-reuse result and its boundary

Fix history \(h\), a continuous readout \(g\), two supported actions \(a,a'\), and a common conditional exogenous law \(\nu_h\). Write the readout outcome as

\[
X(a,e)=g(F(h,a,e)), \qquad
\mu(h,a)=\mathbb E_{e\sim\nu_h}[X(a,e)].
\]

Given factual observation \(x=X(a,e)\), define

\[
T_R(h,a,x,a')=x+\mu(h,a')-\mu(h,a).
\tag{5}
\]

### Proposition 5.1 — Exactness under additive shared disturbance

Suppose that, for readout \(g\), the structural response is additive:

\[
X(a,e)=m(h,a)+q(h,e),
\tag{6}
\]

with the same disturbance term \(q(h,e)\) under \(a\) and \(a'\), and finite second moments. Then

\[
T_R(h,a,X(a,e),a')=X(a',e)
\]

for every supported action pair and almost every \(e\).

**Proof.** From (6), \(\mu(h,a)=m(h,a)+\mathbb E[q(h,E)]\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_R
&=m(h,a)+q(h,e)+m(h,a')+\mathbb E q\\
&\quad-m(h,a)-\mathbb E q\\
&=m(h,a')+q(h,e)=X(a',e).
\end{aligned}
\]
\(\square\)

### Proposition 5.2 — Exact residual risk identity

Without assuming additivity, let \(\Delta(e)=X(a',e)-X(a,e)\), with exact conditional means and finite second moments. Then

\[
T_R-X(a',e)=\mathbb E[\Delta(E)\mid h]-\Delta(e),
\]

and hence

\[
\mathbb E[
\|T_R-X(a',E)\|^2\mid h]
=
\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Var}(\Delta(E)\mid h).
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the residual method's individual-counterfactual error is the variation of the action effect across exogenous disturbances. This identity is exact; it does not say the error is small.

### Proposition 5.3 — Fitted-predictor decomposition

Let \(\widehat\mu(h,a)=\mu(h,a)+e(h,a)\) be fixed independently of the queried episode's disturbance. Then

\[
\widehat T_R-X(a',e)
=
T_R-X(a',e)+e(h,a')-e(h,a).
\]

Under Proposition 5.2, the cross term has zero expectation, so

\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat T_R-X(a',E)\|^2
=
\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Var}(\Delta(E))
+
\|e(h,a')-e(h,a)\|^2.
\tag{8}
\]

The predictor must therefore estimate **action contrasts** accurately; global factual MSE alone is not sufficient.

### 5.1 What is not proved for UCON

The source paper's \(O(h^2)\) short-time rate requires a smooth transition expansion with separate first-order action and disturbance channels, a common disturbance law, adequate state/representation sufficiency, and bounded remainders. UCON state transitions include threshold-triggered PERMIT/DENY events, revocation, role/approval changes, capability-set discontinuities, and discrete agent replanning. These are not automatically smooth in \(h\), and there is no default continuous-time small-step parameter for the discrete policy simulator.

Accordingly:
- Propositions 5.1–5.3 apply to the declared continuous readout under their premises.
- No \(O(h^2)\) guarantee is transferred to categorical policy outcomes or multi-step adaptive trajectories.
- The simulator's exact policy oracle can identify the immediate policy verdict for a supplied request and current state, but not by itself the later agent trajectory under an alternative intervention.
- If action and disturbance interact at first order, if action support fails, or if the representation folds/omits relevant state, residual transport must be rejected or marked unqualified.

## 6. Qualification gates and support-aware abstention

Before reporting a CWM prediction for query \((h,a,a')\), record:

1. **Action support:** \(a'\) lies in the predeclared training/query support for the same task family and policy regime. Out-of-support queries are abstentions, not extrapolation wins.
2. **Conditional exchangeability for mean learning:** after conditioning on \(H_t\), factual action selection is independent of relevant exogenous disturbances/potential outcomes, or the data collection policy explicitly randomizes actions with known nonzero probabilities. This is not inferred from low prediction error.
3. **Overlap:** every queried alternative has positive probability/support in the relevant history/task stratum.
4. **Common disturbance semantics:** branch replay uses the same predeclared exogenous disturbance tape and same disturbance-to-system mapping across action alternatives.
5. **State sufficiency:** \(H_t\) contains the relevant observed state/history; test hidden-state ablations and memory-length sensitivity.
6. **Readout stability:** continuous readout coordinates are predeclared, well-conditioned, and not folded over the query region. Report Jacobian/condition diagnostics where differentiable.
7. **Action–disturbance interaction:** estimate the simulator-only paired action-effect variance diagnostic
\[
\rho(h,a,a')=
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Var}_{E\mid h}
[X(a',E)-X(a,E)]}
{\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Var}_{E\mid h}[X(a',E)]},
\tag{9}
\]
when denominator is positive. This uses paired simulator branches and is a diagnostic, not an input to the factual predictor.
8. **Regime stability:** no policy-version change, authorization threshold crossing, revocation, contact-like state discontinuity, or task-manifest change may be silently pooled into a smooth residual regime.

The model reports per-query status: qualified, qualified-with-warning, or abstain, together with reasons and support distance. A global average must not conceal unsupported subgroups. Selective-risk curves report error versus coverage; comparisons must be made at matched coverage.

The simulator can reveal whether assumptions hold in its own structural implementation. A factual-only real-world dataset cannot generally prove common-disturbance coupling or small action–disturbance interaction. Any real-system transfer claim needs an independent identification argument.

## 7. Model family and baselines

### 7.1 Frozen factual predictors

Use existing G8.4 JEPA and directly supervised matched predictor as primary factual dynamics baselines, with identical input boundaries, train/validation/test partitions, and training budgets. The predictor estimates \(\mu(H_t,a)\) for each supported action.

### 7.2 Counterfactual estimators

- **B0 — Direct factual predictor:** \(\widehat Y(a')=\widehat\mu(H_t,a')\). This is a population/conditional-mean forecast, not an individual counterfactual.
- **B1 — Copy factual outcome:** \(\widehat Y(a')=Y(a)\). A necessary reference for whether the alternative changes the outcome.
- **B2 — Residual transport:** \(Y(a)+\widehat\mu(H_t,a')-\widehat\mu(H_t,a)\), on qualified continuous readouts only.
- **B3 — Readout residual transport:** apply residual transport after the predeclared physical/semantic numeric readout; primary CWM-style estimator for continuous outcomes.
- **B4 — Direct action-conditioned counterfactual head:** trained on factual transitions only, predicts the conditional mean under \(a'\); no observed factual residual is reused.
- **B5 — Paired-supervision oracle ceiling:** trained/evaluated with paired branch outcomes only as an explicitly privileged diagnostic. It is not a fair factual-only baseline and must never be described as deployable from unpaired data.
- **B6 — Structural policy oracle:** computes immediate UCON verdicts exactly for candidate requests; it is the policy-decision reference, not a learned counterfactual world model.
- **B7 — Hybrid policy-aware CWM (proposed):** B3 for qualified continuous variables, B6 for current authorization verdicts, B4 or calibrated event models for discrete future event probabilities, and abstention for unsupported/invalid regimes.

A further action-discriminative predictor baseline may be included if its objective and parameter/training budget can be matched. The recent AD-WM paper is relevant prior work; it motivates action-sensitive prediction but does not by itself establish same-episode, same-disturbance policy counterfactual identification.

### 7.3 Model separation and fairness

- All factual-only estimators use identical factual training episodes.
- No counterfactual branch labels, future UCON oracle labels, or test outcomes enter factual predictor fitting, calibration, representation selection, or threshold selection.
- B5 paired-supervision results are reported in a separate privileged column.
- Parameter counts, optimizer steps, compute, training seeds, and checkpoint-selection rules are retained.
- Any new hybrid-specific learned head receives a matched-capacity ablation; a training-free residual operation is not credited with extra trainable parameters.
- Keep G8.4.8.7.7's decision-time information boundary: no future action or outcome is visible at prediction time.

## 8. Benchmark construction: paired simulator counterfactuals, factual-only model fitting

### 8.1 Unit and branch construction

For each independent parent episode:
1. Generate a factual trajectory under a frozen, versioned behavior policy and policy environment.
2. At predeclared decision anchors \(t\), snapshot complete simulator state, agent state, UCON state/policy version, task manifest, and exogenous random-tape cursor.
3. Record factual action and realized transition.
4. Fork the exact snapshot for each supported alternative action/intervention.
5. Replay each branch with the same exogenous disturbance tape under the common-random-number contract. The alternative branch's agent replanning is allowed to diverge according to the frozen response policy.
6. Store paired branch outcomes and machine-verifiable branch lineage. Paired branch outcomes are evaluation ground truth only for factual-only methods.
7. Assign each parent episode to exactly one data split. All anchors and branches from a parent remain in that split.

The random tape must be indexed by (parent_episode_id, process_name, decision_step, draw_index), not consumed sequentially from a shared mutable PRNG. If two branches request different numbers of random draws, their shared exogenous components still align by key; branch-specific policy/replanning randomness uses separately named streams and is reported.

### 8.2 Action alternatives

Use task-manifest-supported actions only. Candidate bank includes factual submitted action; at least two semantically distinct supported alternatives where feasible; a task-preserving alternative; a policy-boundary/high-risk alternative for stress testing; and a no-op/continue alternative when semantically defined.

Do not compare incomparable actions across task families. Record whether an alternative is supported, task-feasible, policy-PERMIT, policy-DENY, or structurally invalid. The model does not receive oracle verdict as input.

### 8.3 Disjoint partitions

- Train: factual episodes only.
- Validation: factual episodes only; select checkpoints, calibrators, support thresholds, and any abstention threshold.
- Calibration: disjoint factual episodes for probability calibration, if used.
- Counterfactual test: factual inputs plus paired simulator branch outcomes held exclusively for evaluation.
- Stress test: separate held-out mechanisms/parameters, including policy discontinuities and action-dependent disturbance mechanisms.

No parent episode, agent identity, disturbance seed, or branch may cross partitions. Test counterfactual branch outcomes are inaccessible to the model until predictions are frozen.

### 8.4 Required structural stress regimes

1. **Additive shared disturbance:** positive-control regime where residual reuse should be exact up to fitted-mean error.
2. **Smooth separate-channel disturbance:** test action–disturbance interaction as transition duration/decision granularity varies; no small-step rate claim without an actual continuous-time or refined-step construction.
3. **Multiplicative/action-scaled disturbance:** negative-control regime where action contrast depends on disturbance.
4. **Policy threshold discontinuity:** DENY/permit, revocation, role, approval, and capability-boundary transitions.
5. **Hidden-state insufficiency:** omit selected history/state variables and test counterfactual failure.
6. **Representation fold/conditioning:** construct or identify readout regions where latent coordinates are non-injective or ill-conditioned.
7. **Adaptive re-planning:** alternative capability sets change subsequent agent proposals and therefore later state transitions.
8. **Policy shift:** within-archetype parameter shift and reverse-shift conditions consistent with G8.4.8.7.7, plus held-out task/manifest strata.

The benchmark must verify intended positive and negative mechanisms using simulator-only checks before model evaluation.

## 9. Endpoints and metrics

### 9.1 Continuous individual-counterfactual prediction

- Counterfactual \(R^2\), normalized RMSE/MAE, and per-coordinate errors.
- Action-effect error: compare \(\widehat Y(a')-\widehat Y(a)\) with paired truth \(Y(a')-Y(a)\).
- Calibration/coverage for predictive intervals where uncertainty is modeled.
- Error versus action distance, horizon, support distance, and measured \(\rho\).
- Whole-parent bootstrap intervals; never treat multiple branches/anchors from one parent as independent.

### 9.2 Discrete and event counterfactual prediction

- Exact-match accuracy and class-specific precision/recall for event outcomes.
- Brier/log loss and calibration for predicted event probabilities.
- Event-sequence edit distance, time-to-event absolute error, and event omission/insertion rates.
- UCON immediate verdict is separately evaluated against the deterministic policy oracle; it is not a probabilistic JEPA endpoint.

### 9.3 Action selection and regret

Given candidate set \(\mathcal A(H_t)\), define simulator utility \(U(a;\mathbf e)\) for paired branch. The oracle best candidate is \(a^*=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal A}U(a;\mathbf e)\), with deterministic tie handling. The model selects \(\hat a\) from its counterfactual predictions.

\[
\operatorname{Regret}=U(a^*;\mathbf e)-U(\hat a;\mathbf e).
\]

Report regret, goal/task success, UCON-DENY proposal burden, task completion, intervention burden, and policy violations separately. Do not hide security/task trade-offs in an arbitrary scalar utility. Any scalarized utility requires domain-owner-approved weights frozen before test generation.

### 9.4 Selective prediction

For each coverage level, report error among queries the model elects to answer. Compare models at matched coverage. An abstention is not counted as a correct prediction. Also report coverage by task family, action, policy regime, horizon, and structural stress condition.

## 10. Statistical design and inference

- Independent sampling unit: parent episode/agent identity, not branch or anchor.
- Primary comparison: B7 hybrid versus B0 direct factual predictor and B3 residual transport on the qualified continuous-outcome subset.
- Primary continuous endpoint: parent-weighted counterfactual action-effect MSE, standardized by variance of the paired true action effect in the test population. Also report raw-unit errors.
- Primary action-selection endpoint: paired episode-level regret. This is co-primary only if the power analysis and multiplicity plan explicitly include it; otherwise secondary.
- Primary comparisons are paired within parent episode because all methods predict the same factual anchor/action alternatives and are evaluated against the same paired branch truth.
- Resample parent episodes (and any identity clusters) as whole units. Use a predeclared simultaneous max-t or Holm-controlled family for the finite primary contrast set.
- Report per-seed estimates and intervals, not only pooled means. A handful of environment seeds is not a population-level hierarchical sample.
- Sample size is determined by an independent pilot and simulation of the frozen paired analysis. No convenient sample count and no outcome-driven extension.
- Factual predictive metrics and counterfactual metrics are distinct; passing one cannot substitute for the other.

## 11. Negative controls, falsification, and integrity tests

- **Factual-only leakage audit:** no paired branch truth, future oracle verdict, or counterfactual outcome reaches training, calibration, support gating, or model selection.
- **Action-label permutation:** disrupt action/outcome alignment in factual training; action-specific contrasts should degrade.
- **Disturbance-key permutation:** deliberately break common-random-number pairing; simulator ground truth must flag the branch lineage as unpaired.
- **No-action-change control:** querying factual action must reproduce observed factual outcome exactly for residual transport (up to numerical precision).
- **Additive positive control:** with exact conditional means, residual transport must recover paired outcomes within numerical tolerance.
- **Multiplicative negative control:** inject command-scaled disturbances and verify residual error grows with action–disturbance interaction.
- **Policy-boundary negative control:** construct an action whose alternative crosses DENY/permit or revocation boundary; continuous residual transport must not be used to infer categorical verdict.
- **Support violation:** query actions outside factual training support; system must abstain or explicitly label extrapolation, never report qualified status.
- **History ablation:** remove relevant past state and verify state-sufficiency diagnostics detect loss where simulator depends on omitted history.
- **Fold/conditioning stress:** test non-injective/ill-conditioned readouts and verify gate rejects affected coordinates.
- **Branch determinism:** identical snapshot, policy version, alternative action, and exogenous tape reproduce identical branch outputs.
- **UCON dominance:** every executed operation is re-authorized against current UCON state; no counterfactual/planning output can override DENY.
- **Failed runs:** preserve all failures, exclusions, crashes, and artifact mismatches with reasons. No selective deletion.

## 12. Security and system architecture

The architecture has five strictly separated responsibilities:

1. **State/representation encoder:** transforms permitted history and policy context into state representation. It does not receive future labels or oracle outcomes.
2. **Factual action-conditioned dynamics:** predicts factual next-state readouts for supported actions.
3. **Counterfactual evaluator:** applies residual transport only to qualified continuous readouts, predicts discrete event distributions separately, and emits support/assumption status.
4. **Candidate ranker/planner:** ranks candidate actions using predicted outcomes and explicit task/security constraints. It cannot issue authorization.
5. **UCON policy enforcement:** sole authority for each actual tool request; re-evaluates current subject, object, action, context, obligations, and policy version. It may deny any selected action.

Planner output is a proposal to the orchestration layer, never a permit token. A candidate predicted safe still passes through UCON. If UCON denies, the operation is not executed. Prediction failure, uncertainty, unsupported action, stale state, or model unavailability must not weaken authorization.

## 13. Relationship to G8.4.8.8 randomized intervention utility

G8.4.8.8 remains the randomized four-arm strategy-level ITT evaluation: UCON-only; DENY-triggered restrictive overlay; JEPA-triggered restrictive overlay; supervised-triggered restrictive overlay.

G8.4.8.9 adds counterfactual model evaluation and action comparison. It may inform which interventions are worth testing, but it cannot replace randomization for estimating the effect of assigning the G8.4.8.8 strategies.

A counterfactual estimate for one episode does not identify the average treatment effect, the effect among actually triggered episodes, or the strategy-level ITT. Conversely, the G8.4.8.8 ITT does not establish accurate individual counterfactual predictions.

If a CWM-based planner is later evaluated as a fifth strategy, this requires a separately powered and preregistered trial amendment; do not add it post hoc to the existing four-arm trial.
