## 1. Decision

The primary decision-time comparison does **not** support a general or stable JEPA advantage over the directly supervised recursive control. Mean Brier differences are close to zero, their signs vary across seeds, and the 15 archetype × security-family cells show substantial heterogeneity.

The corrected one-window-per-episode endpoint is the primary scientific result. All-overlapping-window results are retained only as secondary descriptive diagnostics. In some cases, they materially change the apparent comparison and must not be used to claim a decision-time benefit.

This is evidence from the specified synthetic controlled-shift environment only. It does not establish real-agent transfer, intervention utility, or production security assurance.

## 3. Frozen protocol and primary estimand

The implementation preserves the G8.4.8.7.5 models, generator, episode-disjoint partitions, calibration algorithm, training budget, and test selection. It corrects the evaluation population:

- Five seeds: 20261120–20261124.
- Five synthetic behavior archetypes × three security families.
- 300 episodes per archetype/family/rho cell.
- Training support: rho ∈ {0.20, 0.40, 0.60}; shifted test rho=0.85; reverse diagnostic rho=0.05.
- Horizons H ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}; 32 Monte Carlo action rollouts.
- Exactly one `start_step=0` forecast window per episode for primary calibration and primary test evaluation.
- 4,500 primary test episodes per seed, split and horizon.
- Paired percentile bootstrap: 2,000 replicates, resampling whole episodes (one primary row per episode).
- Primary contrast: JEPA E2 Brier − supervised E2 Brier; negative favours JEPA.
- All overlapping windows are a separate secondary analysis.

The focused tests verify that the selector rejects missing or duplicate step-0 starts, and that the primary row count equals the number of episodes. Formal UCON remains the sole authorization authority; predictive outputs cannot authorize an operation or override DENY.

## 6. Subgroup audit

All 15 behavior-archetype × security-family cells are retained per seed, split and horizon (300 episodes per cell). The subgroup results are heterogeneous; neither model wins uniformly.

Examples from the shifted condition:
- At H1, JEPA has higher mean Brier in all five seeds for the denial-adaptive-explorer / IAM cell (mean Δ +0.00533), while it has lower mean Brier in four of five seeds for the goal-directed / SoD cell (mean Δ −0.01202).
- At H8, JEPA has lower mean Brier in four of five seeds for goal-directed / data (mean Δ −0.01750), but higher mean Brier in four of five seeds for goal-directed / SoD (mean Δ +0.02363).
- At H8, the denial-adaptive-explorer / data cell favours the supervised control in four of five seeds (mean Δ +0.00676), while denial-adaptive-explorer / IAM favours JEPA in four of five (mean Δ −0.00642).

These are descriptive subgroup patterns, not confirmatory family-wide claims. Small per-cell sample sizes and substantial seed ranges require caution. Full cell-level metrics are in `runs[*].tests[*][h].decision_time_primary.by_archetype_family`.

## 7. Why the sliding-window result is secondary

The old G8.4.8.7.5 headline pooled all overlapping windows even though calibration used one step-0 window per episode. This stage confirms that the estimand choice can change the comparison.

For seed 20261120, H2, shifted rho=0.85:
- Correct decision-time primary: JEPA-minus-supervised Brier = **+0.000670**, 95% paired episode CI [−0.000061, +0.001458].
- Secondary all-window analysis: difference = **−0.008224**, 95% paired episode CI [−0.010316, −0.006326].

The secondary analysis therefore suggests a much larger JEPA advantage for this seed/horizon than the matched decision-time endpoint. It must not be used as the primary scientific verdict. At H8 the episode length yields one forecast window per episode, so the primary and all-window populations coincide.

## 8. Model comparability and limitations

- JEPA E2: 852 trainable parameters (1,260 stored including EMA target).
- Supervised E2: 776 trainable parameters (91.1% of JEPA's trainable parameter count).
- Both use the same stated seed and training schedule; the controls are close in size but not exactly parameter-matched.
- The supervised control directly predicts next observable context and security targets, then re-encodes predicted context for recursive rollout; it has no EMA target encoder.
- Five synthetic behavior archetypes and one scalar within-archetype mixture parameter limit the behavioral diversity.
- E1 privileged known-action results are reported in G8.4.8.7.4 and are not a deployable comparator.
- No real-agent transfer, intervention utility, production authorization claim, or proof of JEPA necessity is established.
