# G5.1 — Workflow-level estimand and sampling model

**Status:** Defined; deployment-distribution assumptions remain to be discharged.

## 1. The unit of inference is a complete workflow episode

A tool call is not an independent statistical observation. Tool calls within one agent workflow share state, identity, resources, context, and causal history. Treating each call as IID would generally understate uncertainty.

Define a workflow episode \(W\) as one complete, bounded agent task from a declared admission boundary to a terminal outcome or timeout. The episode contains all dependent actions, observations, policy checks, and external-effect events.

For episode \(i\), define:

\[
Z_i=(X_i,Q_i,Y_i,V_i),
\]

where \(X_i\) is pre-execution context, \(Q_i\) is a frozen predictive assurance score, \(Y_i\) is a trajectory-level adverse outcome, and \(V_i\) is version/audit metadata.

The episode-level outcome is:

\[
Y_i=\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{Bad}_{\mathcal P_v}(\tau_i)=1\}.
\]

The bad event must be specified before data collection. It may include a policy violation, protected-flow violation, invalid delegation, obligation breach, or formally defined unsafe trajectory.

## 2. Enforcement-induced censoring

Production UCON enforcement is designed to block prohibited effects. Therefore, observing no completed violation after enforcement does not prove that the proposed workflow was intrinsically safe. The intervention censors the counterfactual outcome.

Distinguish:
1. \(Y^{cf}\): whether the candidate workflow violates the trajectory policy under a specified reference execution semantics, evaluated in sandbox or controlled replay;
2. \(Y^{int}\): whether the runtime system had to deny, restrict, or escalate the workflow;
3. \(Y^{prod}\): whether an actual prohibited external effect occurred in production.

These are not interchangeable. The primary G5 estimand is \(Y^{cf}\). \(Y^{int}\) is an operational surrogate measuring intervention frequency. \(Y^{prod}\) is censored by formal enforcement and must not be used naively as the calibration label.

The guarantee obtained from sandbox labels applies to the sandbox/reference execution distribution. A production claim requires a separately justified simulator-to-real transfer argument or safe prospective observation design.

## 3. Fixed-action versus policy-induced episode

For a fixed candidate plan \(\pi_i\):

\[
Y_i^{cf}=\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{Bad}_{\mathcal P_v}(\tau_i(\pi_i))\}.
\]

If the agent revises its plan in response to policy changes or observations, the target must include an action-selection mechanism \(\mu_\psi\):

\[
Y_i^{beh}=\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{Bad}_{\mathcal P_v}(\tau_i\sim T,\mu_\psi)\}.
\]

These targets require different datasets and must not be pooled without justification.

## 4. Population estimand

Let \(Q,Y\) follow the target episode distribution. For a fixed threshold \(t\), define:

\[
S_t=\mathbf 1\{Q\le t\},\qquad \pi(t)=\Pr(S_t=1),
\]

and, when \(\pi(t)>0\),

\[
R(t)=\Pr(Y=1\mid Q\le t).
\]

The goal is to choose a selective workflow-admission threshold with \(R(t)\le\epsilon\) while retaining high \(\pi(t)\).

This is a marginal selective-risk guarantee over the specified population. It is not a pointwise guarantee for every workflow context.

## 5. Sampling assumptions

The basic theorem uses independent workflow episodes:

\[
Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathbb P_{target}.
\]

Distinct invocation IDs do not ensure independence. Episodes may remain dependent through shared users, tenants, resources, persistent agent memory, or temporal infrastructure.

If dependencies cannot be credibly ignored, define a higher-level independent cluster \(C_i\) and calibrate a cluster-level event:

\[
Y(C_i)=\mathbf 1\{\text{at least one bad workflow in cluster }C_i\}.
\]

The resulting guarantee is cluster-level, not automatically per workflow.

## 6. Frozen model and policy context

Before calibration, freeze JEPA parameters, action encoder, security decoder, rollout horizon, score construction, policy version, tool/environment version scope, outcome definition, and candidate threshold grid.

A model, policy, tool, or label-definition update may change the joint distribution of \((Q,Y)\). Recalibration or a justified transport method is required.

## 7. Guarantee scope

Under the IID target-episode assumption and a valid calibration procedure:

\[
\Pr_{\mathcal D_{cal}}
[\hat t=NULL\ \lor\ R_{\mathbb P_{target}}(\hat t)\le\epsilon]\ge1-\delta.
\]

The probability is over the calibration sample. It controls population risk among future accepted episodes under the same target distribution. It does not guarantee every episode is safe, eliminate formal enforcement, or automatically transfer sandbox risk to production.
