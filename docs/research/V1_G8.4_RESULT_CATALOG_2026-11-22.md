# V1 — G8.4 empirical result catalog

**Snapshot date:** 22 November 2026 (UTC)  
**Purpose:** consolidate what the existing G8.4 experiments actually establish, what they do not establish, and where the supporting evidence lives.  
**Scope:** G8.4.1–G8.4.9, including the G8.4.8 uncertain-action and policy-shift sequence.  
**Implementation:** none. This catalog is a synthesis of existing result dossiers and retained artifacts. The only independent binary-level reanalysis in this V1 pass is the PR #45/#46 artifact provenance audit.

## 1. Overall empirical conclusion

The current G8.4 evidence supports the existence of predictive signal in selected synthetic settings, but **does not establish a stable JEPA-specific advantage over strong supervised or historical baselines**.

The evidence becomes progressively more rigorous:

1. Early JEPA benchmarks showed promising predictive signal but also clear supervised-baseline wins on compositional generalization and weak long-horizon performance.
2. Under uncertain actions, the privileged known-action diagnostic (E1) is substantially stronger than predicted-action rollout (E2). Joint action/state learning improves over the post-hoc JEPA latent action head at shorter horizons, but does not solve long-horizon discrimination.
3. Under held-out behavior-policy shift, results are heterogeneous. Historical frequency remains a strong baseline; the learned action model does not generalize uniformly.
4. The matched recursive supervised comparator removes the basis for attributing the observed predictive signal to JEPA specifically. H1/H2 generally favor supervised prediction; H4/H8 are near ties with seed and subgroup reversals.
5. Calibration findings exposed a mismatch between calibration and evaluation temporal support. Decision-time evaluation corrected the primary estimand to one start-step-0 forecast per episode. The resulting JEPA-minus-supervised Brier differences are very small and mixed across seeds.
6. Intervention and counterfactual work remain protocol/design assets, not validated empirical contributions.

This is a synthetic controlled-shift research program. It does not establish transfer to real autonomous agents, causal intervention benefit, production security assurance, or a population-level JEPA advantage.

## 3. Key protocol distinctions that must survive publication

### 3.1 E1 is privileged; E2 is the decision-time candidate

- **E1:** receives the realized future action sequence. It is a diagnostic upper-reference condition, not deployable.
- **E2:** receives only initial context and generates future actions from its own predictor. It is the relevant predicted-action rollout, but its performance is materially weaker.
- Monte Carlo variation over sampled action sequences is not epistemic uncertainty.

Any paper that reports E1 as if it were a deployable forecast would misstate the information boundary.

### 3.2 Historical and supervised comparators are not optional

The strongest negative findings occur when JEPA is compared with:
- historical-frequency action prediction;
- first-order Markov prediction;
- past-only supervised context prediction;
- matched recursive supervised state-dynamics prediction.

A paper must retain these baselines. A comparison only against majority or a weak post-hoc JEPA head is insufficient.

### 3.3 Primary and secondary evaluation populations differ

PR #45's primary estimand is one start-step-0 forecast per episode. The overlapping-window analysis is secondary because it does not match the calibration population. The reported differences can change materially when the evaluation population changes. The primary claim must follow the frozen decision-time endpoint.

### 3.4 Uncertainty and inference boundaries

- Episode-level resampling is used to avoid treating overlapping windows/transitions as independent.
- Five synthetic seeds do not form a population-level hierarchical confidence interval.
- LOPO folds share overlapping training-policy populations and are not independent samples of real agent policies.
- The scalar rho mixture is a controlled synthetic policy-parameter shift, not a broad distribution over agent behaviors.
- Formal UCON remains the only authorization authority in every stage.

## 5. Evidence status and publication implications

**Can be stated now, with the synthetic-scope qualifier:**
- The G8.4 benchmark pipeline and several independent validation gates have been implemented and exercised.
- The synthetic uncertain-action setting exposes a substantial gap between privileged known-action forecasting and predicted-action rollout.
- Strong supervised and historical baselines match or outperform JEPA on key action and recursive-risk endpoints.
- Decision-time matched JEPA/supervised differences are small, horizon-dependent and heterogeneous.
- Calibration behavior depends on the temporal support used for fitting and evaluation.

**Must not be claimed:**
- JEPA is superior to supervised learning for agent security prediction.
- JEPA robustly generalizes to unseen real agent policies.
- The predictor reduces actual security breaches or losses.
- Predictive intervention has a measured causal benefit.
- Counterfactual policy worlds are identified or validated.
- Predictive outputs provide authorization or security guarantees.

**Potential contribution worth preserving:** a rigorous empirical characterization of the information and evaluation problems that arise when using predictive state models for security anticipation under uncertain actions and changing agent policies, combined with a strict separation between predictive advice and authoritative UCON enforcement. Novelty still requires a literature audit; negative empirical results alone do not establish novelty.
