# G8.4.1 — JEPA role contract for predictive usage control

**Status:** architecture contract and executable interface scaffold; no trained JEPA or empirical predictive result is claimed.

## Research question

Does an action-conditioned Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) learn representations that improve prediction of security-relevant future trajectory properties in compositional autonomous-agent workflows, compared with matched predictive alternatives, while leaving authorization and enforcement exclusively to UCON?

JEPA is the principal modelling hypothesis, not a theoretically necessary component. A null or adverse comparison is a valid research outcome.

## 1. System-level separation

The architecture contains three planes that must not be collapsed:

1. **Predictive representation plane:** context encoder, action encoder, predictor, EMA target encoder, and optional security-feature decoder.
2. **Assurance plane:** uncertainty estimation, calibration, trajectory-level policy-property evaluation, and selective intervention logic.
3. **Authority plane:** the authoritative UCON policy evaluator and execution gateway.

The predictor produces evidence about possible future states. It does not produce an authorization certificate, mutate policy state, satisfy obligations, or authorize tool execution.

## 2. JEPA roles and information boundaries

Let the observed history available at decision time be
\[
H_t=(o_{0:t},a_{0:t-1},e_{0:t}),
\]
where observations and environment events are those actually available at time t. Let \(G_t\) denote the authoritative governance state, and let \(a_t\) be a proposed typed action.

| Role | Definition / input | Output | Responsibility and restriction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context encoder \(E_\theta\) | Past observation/action history \(H_t\) | Context latent \(s_t\) | Compresses the available past; must not receive future observations or future labels |
| Action encoder \(E_a\) | Candidate action and permitted current action context \((a_t,G_t^{obs})\) | Action latent \(z_t^a\) | Makes prediction action-conditioned; must not receive the realized future |
| Predictor \(P_\phi\) | \((s_t,z_t^a,k)\) | Predicted latent \(\hat s_{t+k}\) | Predicts future representation at horizon k; it is not a policy evaluator |
| EMA target encoder \(E_{\bar\theta}\) | Realized future observation/history available after the outcome | Target latent \(s^*_{t+k}\) | Supplies a slowly moving self-supervised target; target branch is stop-gradient |
| Security-feature decoder \(D_\psi\) (auxiliary) | Predicted latent \(\hat s_{t+k}\) | Explicit future security-feature estimates | Exposes policy-relevant quantities; it must not be mistaken for the authoritative policy state |
| Uncertainty/calibration module \(C_\omega\) (separate) | Predictions and held-out calibration data | Risk/uncertainty estimate with declared scope | Supports selective-risk claims only after independent calibration; not part of JEPA's latent prediction guarantee |
| Policy-property evaluator \(Q_{\mathcal P}\) | Explicit trajectory/features and versioned policy semantics | Predicted property assessment | Applies specified security semantics; it is not the JEPA loss and must not be replaced by latent distance |
| UCON evaluator and gateway | Current authoritative \((G_t,a_t,\mathcal P_v)\) | Formal decision and mediated effect | Sole authority for current admission and execution |

The target encoder is not a second predictor. It encodes the realized future target for representation learning. The predictor is trained to forecast that target from past context and candidate action.

## 3. Core equations

For a one-step action-conditioned prediction:
\[
s_t=E_\theta(H_t),\qquad
z_t^a=E_a(a_t,G_t^{obs}),
\]
\[
\hat s_{t+1}=P_\phi(s_t,z_t^a),\qquad
s^*_{t+1}=\operatorname{sg}\left(E_{\bar\theta}(H_{t+1})\right).
\]

A generic latent objective is
\[
\mathcal L_{JEPA}=d(\hat s_{t+1},s^*_{t+1}),
\]
where the distance, normalization, masking strategy, and collapse-prevention mechanism must be specified by the concrete implementation. This equation alone is not a complete training algorithm and does not imply that security-relevant features are predictable.

A security auxiliary objective may be used:
\[
\mathcal L_{sec}=\ell(D_\psi(\hat s_{t+1}),y^{sec}_{t+1}),
\qquad
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{JEPA}+\lambda_{sec}\mathcal L_{sec}.
\]
The label \(y^{sec}\) is constructed by the versioned policy/trajectory oracle from realized, explicitly recorded state and events. It is a training/evaluation target only; it must not enter the predictor input.

For multi-step prediction, the model must state whether it predicts each horizon directly or recursively rolls predicted latents forward. These choices have different error propagation and must be separately evaluated.

## 4. EMA target update

A standard target update is
\[
\bar\theta\leftarrow \tau\bar\theta+(1-\tau)\theta,
\quad 0<\tau<1.
\]
The target branch is evaluated without gradient updates. The exact update cadence, initialization, target projection/predictor asymmetry, and any stop-gradient points are implementation choices that must be versioned and ablated. The EMA mechanism is not itself evidence of representation quality.

## 5. Prediction target: future policy-evaluable trajectory

The study target is not an action label alone. For a candidate action sequence \(\mathbf a_{t:t+K-1}\), the target is a future trajectory
\[
\tau_{t:t+K}=(G_t,a_t,G_{t+1},\ldots,a_{t+K-1},G_{t+K}).
\]
The model may predict latent states, but evaluation must decode and score explicit security-relevant outcomes: authorization-relevant attributes, approval/SoD state, data classification/clearance, recipient/scope, revocation/version freshness, obligation status, and whether/when a policy violation occurs. Feature availability is family-specific and must be declared.

The policy oracle labels the realized trajectory under a named policy version. Historical replay under the actual policy and future model-based prediction under candidate actions are distinct tasks. A fixed action sequence does not model how an agent adapts its plan to a changed policy.

## 6. Runtime decision composition

Let \(D_F(G_t,a_t,\mathcal P_v)\) be the authoritative formal UCON decision. Let \(R_J\) be the predictive advisory output. The non-bypass condition is
\[
D_F=\mathsf{DENY}\implies D_O=\mathsf{DENY}.
\]
The predictor may trigger an additional deny/restrict/escalate/abstain action only through a separately specified and validated operational controller. It may never turn formal DENY into ALLOW. A low predicted risk is not evidence of current authorization.

## 7. Leakage and validity rules

- Future observations, future actions, future policy labels, and oracle violation labels are target-side data only.
- Candidate action and its declared current context are the only action-side inputs.
- Train/validation/calibration/test partitions are episode-level at minimum; compositional holdouts must prevent the target action combination from appearing in training.
- Normalization, vocabularies, calibration, and threshold selection are fitted on permitted training/validation/calibration partitions only.
- Policy version, tool version, tenant/principal grouping, and temporal cutoffs must be recorded.
- If the environment is partially observed, the predictor must not be trained/evaluated as if latent state were directly observed. State estimation uncertainty is part of the problem.
- No latent loss, representation similarity, or next-state accuracy may substitute for security-feature metrics.

## 8. Required comparisons and ablations

The JEPA claim must be tested against matched:
1. supervised transition predictor with comparable inputs, output targets, capacity, and training budget;
2. probabilistic state-space / sequence predictor where feasible;
3. history-only predictor without candidate-action conditioning;
4. action-only or context-only ablations;
5. direct security-feature prediction baseline;
6. reactive formal UCON baseline, which remains authoritative.

Report parameter count, data, compute, tuning budget, and seeds. Evaluate one-step and multi-step feature error, trajectory violation prediction, compositional generalization, calibration/selective risk, false interventions, lead time, latency, throughput, and utility. Null results must be retained.

## 9. Acceptance criteria for this increment

- Every component has a typed role, explicit input/output boundary, and non-authority statement.
- The executable scaffold rejects future/label fields at the predictor-input boundary.
- The target-side sample is structurally distinct from the predictor input.
- Tests verify action conditioning, target/input separation, and formal-denial dominance.
- No trained model, predictive gain, security guarantee, or JEPA novelty is claimed by this specification increment.

## 10. Open obligations

G8.4.1 does not implement neural encoders or train a JEPA. The next increments must implement a reproducible model, target EMA, objective, and optimizer; prove data-flow separation in the training pipeline; construct policy-evaluable trajectory targets; then run the matched controlled benchmark. Literature review and novelty analysis remain required before publication claims.
