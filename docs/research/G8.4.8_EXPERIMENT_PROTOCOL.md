# G8.4.8 — Forecasting security consequences under uncertain agent actions

**Status:** protocol specification; no G8.4.8 model or empirical result is claimed.  
**Depends on:** G8.4.1–G8.4.7, especially the fixed-length policy-conditioned trajectories in G8.4.7.  
**Authority boundary:** formal UCON remains the sole authorization and execution authority.

## 1. Research question

Can an action-conditioned JEPA anticipate security-relevant future trajectory outcomes when the agent's future action sequence is not known at prediction time, and does this anticipation provide measurable operational value beyond reactive formal UCON?

The key distinction from G8.4.6 is that future actions are no longer supplied to every forecast. The study separates privileged known-action forecasting from deployable predicted-action forecasting and joint action/state forecasting.

JEPA is a modelling hypothesis, not a required winner. Null or adverse results are valid outcomes.

## 2. Scope and non-goals

This increment evaluates synthetic, policy-conditioned IAM, sensitive-data and separation-of-duties workflows. It does not establish real-agent representativeness, production security, causal benefit, general JEPA superiority, or an authorization capability.

Out of scope:
- allowing a predictor to authorize, execute, or override an action;
- using future actions, future observations, denial labels, or oracle outputs as inference-time features;
- claiming that an oracle-action experiment is deployable;
- interpreting a prediction score as a formal policy decision;
- treating a reactive UCON baseline as a weak predictor rather than the authoritative current-action gate.

## 3. Hypotheses and estimands

### Primary hypotheses

- **H1 (action forecast):** a past-only agent-policy model predicts the next action better than declared non-learning and simple-history baselines on unseen episodes.
- **H2 (uncertain-action security forecast):** a JEPA receiving predicted action distributions/sequences produces useful future security forecasts under action uncertainty, measured against the same realized future trajectories.
- **H3 (joint prediction):** joint action/state latent prediction improves at least one predeclared trajectory-level security metric over a matched factorized action-then-state model, without unacceptable degradation in action prediction or calibration.
- **H4 (operational value):** a separately specified advisory intervention policy can improve a predeclared cost-sensitive anticipation metric over observation-only monitoring, while every current action remains subject to formal UCON.
- **H5 (policy generalization):** performance is retained on behavior policies entirely excluded from model fitting and model selection.

H1–H5 are empirical hypotheses, not assumptions. No hypothesis is accepted from training loss or CI alone.

### Primary estimands

1. Next-action negative log-likelihood and top-1 accuracy on held-out episodes/policies.
2. Horizon-specific probability and ranking quality for any DENY in the next h transitions.
3. Horizon-specific error for explicit future security/workflow features.
4. Episode-level false-intervention rate, missed-event rate and warning lead time at thresholds fixed without test data.
5. Paired episode-cluster differences between methods on the same held-out episodes.

Report horizons h = 1, 2, 4 and 8 only where a complete fixed-length window exists. Since G8.4.7 episodes are exactly eight transitions, the origin and target alignment must be stated explicitly; no window may cross an episode boundary.

## 4. Dataset and policy design

### 4.1 Source of truth

Use `experiments/G8.4/policy_trajectory_dataset.py` and its tests as the starting point. Do not use the older first-DENY-censored `stateful_trajectory_dataset.py` generator as the G8.4.8 trajectory source.

Preserve the G8.4.7 version and results unchanged. G8.4.8 must introduce a separately versioned dataset generator/schema and record its source revision, policy semantics version, action vocabulary, feature schema, seeds and hashes.

### 4.2 Fixed-length transition semantics

Each episode must contain the declared number of transitions, including after DENY. A denied action:
- does not mutate protected workflow state;
- records attempt history and denial feedback;
- permits the behavior policy to choose its next action;
- records the actual attempted action and the independent oracle's decision.

The batch policy oracle and online enforcement gate must remain independently implemented and differentially checked on every generated transition. The learned predictors must never replace either.

### 4.3 Behavior-policy coverage

Retain the three G8.4.7 policies (goal-directed, uniform, retrying adversary) as compatibility baselines. Add at least two predeclared, substantively distinct policies before confirmatory runs, for example:
- **prerequisite-blind planner:** follows a fixed workflow ordering but does not inspect governance preconditions;
- **denial-adaptive explorer:** changes its next action after denial using only observed denial feedback and a fixed stochastic rule.

These examples must be precisely specified, tested, and frozen before data generation. Do not tune them after inspecting test performance. The added policies remain synthetic stress policies, not a claimed sample of real agent behavior.

### 4.4 Splits

Provide two complementary protocols:

**Episode-disjoint in-policy protocol**
- Deterministic train/validation/calibration/test partitions at the episode level.
- Every episode belongs to exactly one partition.
- Scaling and feature transformations fit on training only.
- Model selection and stopping use validation only.
- Probability calibration and operating-threshold selection use a distinct calibration partition (or a clearly separated validation subpartition).
- Test is evaluated once after the pipeline is frozen.

**Leave-one-policy-out (LOPO) protocol**
- Hold out one complete behavior-policy identity from all model training, hyperparameter selection, calibration fitting and threshold selection.
- Train on episodes from the remaining policies; test only on episodes from the held-out policy.
- Rotate across each predeclared policy, reporting each fold separately and a macro-average.
- Never pass the held-out policy identity as an input feature.
- Keep episode groups intact and prevent any trajectory from crossing folds.

With only the original three behavior policies, LOPO is a narrow synthetic stress test. The expanded policy set is required before drawing even a limited policy-generalization conclusion.

### 4.5 Input/target contract

At decision time t, the model may receive only the observable history and current governance observations available by t. Candidate/current action is available only in the known-action state-transition condition where explicitly declared. Future action sequence, future observations, denial outcomes, oracle reasons and security labels are target-side only.

Policy identity, episode ID, seed, partition, future action names and oracle reasons are metadata/grouping/evaluation fields; they must not enter predictor features.

## 5. Experimental conditions

All methods are evaluated on identical held-out episodes, target horizons and realized trajectories.

### E1 — Known-action-sequence forecasting (privileged diagnostic)

Supply the realized future action sequence to the state/trajectory forecaster. This is an oracle-information condition that measures the conditional state-forecasting ceiling when the action plan is known. Label it **oracle-action / privileged**, never deployable. It must not be compared to E2 as if the information sets were equal.

### E2 — Predicted-action-sequence forecasting (factorized)

1. Fit an agent-policy model using past-only observations to predict a distribution over the next action.
2. Generate future action distributions autoregressively without consulting realized future actions.
3. Pass predicted action distributions (or samples from a declared predictive distribution) to the JEPA state forecaster.
4. Propagate action uncertainty through the forecast. Report both expected-risk estimates and distributional/quantile summaries where implemented.
5. Do not silently replace predicted actions with ground-truth actions after the first step.

The action model must be compared with at least a majority/history-frequency baseline and a simple first-order Markov baseline. Report action NLL/log-loss, top-1/top-k accuracy, Brier score for action probabilities, and exact sequence accuracy as a diagnostic.

### E3 — Joint action/state latent prediction

Implement a model that predicts future action and future state/security representation jointly from past-only context. Specify whether action and state heads share a latent, how future targets are stop-gradient/target-side, how the joint objective is weighted, and how uncertainty is represented.

Compare it with:
- E2 factorized action-then-state prediction;
- capacity/compute-matched supervised joint action/state prediction;
- context-only and action-history-only ablations;
- the JEPA known-action diagnostic from E1.

The joint model must not receive future actions or security labels at inference. The joint loss must not permit target leakage through shared preprocessing or batch construction.

### E4 — Reactive formal UCON baseline

At every current decision point, run the authoritative formal policy oracle on the current governance state and proposed current action. It must deny every action that violates the current policy and admit only those satisfying it.

This baseline is not asked to forecast an unknown future plan. Report its exact current-action decision correctness against the independent oracle, enforcement latency, and any downstream outcomes. Predictive methods may provide an advisory warning or request a separately authorized restriction/escalation, but cannot convert DENY to ALLOW or execute tools.

### E5 — Held-out behavior-policy generalization

Run LOPO folds over all predeclared policies. Report per-policy action prediction, security forecasting, calibration, false-intervention and lead-time metrics. Do not hide a failed policy fold in an aggregate mean.

## 6. Baselines and ablations

Required:
1. majority action predictor;
2. first-order Markov action predictor;
3. past-only supervised action-policy model;
4. factorized action predictor + JEPA state predictor;
5. joint action/state JEPA;
6. capacity/compute-matched supervised joint predictor;
7. context/history-only predictor;
8. known-action privileged JEPA diagnostic;
9. reactive formal UCON gate.

Record parameter counts, training updates, seeds, input information, compute budget, stopping rules and hyperparameter search budget. Where exact matching is impossible, state the mismatch and add sensitivity analysis.

## 7. Metrics and statistical analysis

### Action forecasting
- multiclass log-loss / negative log-likelihood;
- top-1 and top-k accuracy;
- action-probability Brier score and calibration;
- exact sequence match and per-horizon action accuracy.

### Security forecasting
- AUROC and AUPRC with event prevalence;
- Brier score and reliability/calibration diagnostics;
- security-feature MSE/MAE by feature and policy family;
- horizon-specific any-DENY prediction;
- event timing and warning lead time.

### Operational advisory value
- false interventions per 1,000 decisions and per episode;
- missed violations and intervention precision/recall;
- warning lead time distribution;
- abstention/coverage and risk-versus-coverage curves;
- latency and throughput;
- cost-sensitive utility under predeclared false-positive, false-negative and delay costs.

A warning counts as useful only if it precedes the relevant event by the declared minimum lead time and is generated from information available at that warning time. The formal UCON gate is evaluated independently on every actual action.

### Uncertainty
- Use multiple fixed seeds.
- Use paired episode-cluster bootstrap intervals for method differences, stratified by policy fold and seed where appropriate.
- Resample whole episodes, not rows or overlapping windows.
- Report per-seed and per-policy results; macro-average policies so large policies do not dominate.
- Correct or explicitly control multiplicity for confirmatory comparisons and multiple horizons.
- Do not interpret across-seed standard deviation as a population confidence interval.
- State the independent sampling unit and the limited synthetic population to which intervals apply.

### Thresholds and calibration
No test-derived threshold. Fit calibrators and select advisory thresholds on the dedicated calibration partition only. Freeze them before test. Report raw and calibrated metrics. If a policy is held out under LOPO, its labels cannot be used to calibrate that fold; use a predeclared cross-policy calibration strategy or report calibration as unavailable for that fold.

## 8. Leakage, correctness and adversarial gates

Automated tests must establish:
- future action arrays are absent from E2/E3 model inputs;
- changing future action labels while holding past observations fixed cannot change E2/E3 predictions;
- changing future security labels cannot change predictions;
- E1 alone receives the declared oracle future-action input;
- E2 autoregressive generation never reads the realized next action;
- E3 joint targets are target-side only and stop-gradient where applicable;
- policy IDs, episode IDs, seeds, oracle reasons and partition labels are excluded from features;
- train/validation/calibration/test episode sets are pairwise disjoint;
- LOPO held-out policy is absent from all fitting and tuning partitions;
- all episodes have exact fixed length and all forecast windows are episode-local;
- independent oracle and online gate agree on all generated transitions;
- DENY preserves protected state and records feedback;
- formal DENY dominates every predictive/advisory output in runtime composition.

Include negative controls: shuffled action labels, shuffled episode-policy assignment, future-action leakage injection that tests must detect, and a context-only model. A negative control that unexpectedly performs well must trigger investigation, not be ignored.

## 9. Runtime authority contract

Let F(G_t,a_t,P_v) be the formal UCON decision and J_t be any JEPA/action-model advisory. For every actual current action:

F(G_t,a_t,P_v) = DENY  implies  FinalDecision_t = DENY.

A predictive ALLOW, low risk score, missing prediction, timeout, malformed output or model failure must never bypass formal UCON. The predictor may only produce an advisory. Any additional deny/restrict/escalate mechanism requires a separately specified, fail-safe controller and must be evaluated for false interventions and operational cost.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

G8.4.8 is not complete until all of the following are met:

1. Protocol and hypotheses frozen before confirmatory implementation/results.
2. Versioned fixed-length dataset with at least five predeclared behavior policies and exact provenance.
3. Episode-disjoint and LOPO split tests pass.
4. E1–E5 are implemented with explicit information-set contracts.
5. E2/E3 future-action and future-label leakage tests pass.
6. Action-model and JEPA/supervised baselines are fairly compared.
7. Multiple-seed experiments, per-policy results and paired episode-cluster uncertainty are retained.
8. Calibration/threshold selection is independent of test.
9. Reactive formal UCON remains authoritative and DENY dominance is tested.
10. All results, commands, environment, artifact hashes, limitations and commit SHA are documented.
11. CI passes on the exact final PR head; no pending required check is treated as success.
12. A scientific verdict states which hypotheses are supported, rejected or unresolved.

Passing unit tests or CI alone does not establish predictive or operational benefit.

## 11. Falsification and stop rules

Stop or downgrade claims if:
- predicted-action performance collapses under LOPO;
- a context-only or simple Markov baseline matches or exceeds the proposed models;
- E2/E3 results depend on future-action or target leakage;
- joint JEPA fails to beat the matched supervised joint baseline on predeclared primary estimands;
- calibration is poor or unavailable for the claimed deployment setting;
- false interventions erase any warning benefit;
- a warning is not early enough to precede the violation;
- the result changes materially under policy mix, seed, or horizon sensitivity;
- the runtime composition permits predictive output to override formal DENY.

A negative or inconclusive verdict is a valid research outcome. Do not add model complexity solely to reverse an adverse result.
