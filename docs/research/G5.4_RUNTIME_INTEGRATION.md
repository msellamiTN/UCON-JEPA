# G5.4 — Runtime integration of trajectory risk certification

## 1. Frozen workflow admission object

At workflow admission, construct:

\[
X=(H_t,G_t^{sec},\mathcal P_v,\pi,K,\mathcal V).
\]

The JEPA-based pipeline computes a scalar score:

\[
Q_\theta(X)=g_\omega(
\widehat{\mathcal T}_{t:t+K},
u_\theta(X),
G_t^{sec}
).
\]

Lower scores indicate greater predicted safety. \(Q_\theta\) need not be a calibrated probability. Its role is to rank candidate workflow episodes for selective admission.

The exact score function, rollout horizon, predictor version, policy version, and tool/environment versions are frozen before calibration.

## 2. Certification artifact

The calibration artifact stores:

- model and encoder hashes;
- policy version and policy semantic hash;
- tool/environment version scope;
- score-function version;
- outcome definition and labeler version;
- independent pilot threshold grid;
- calibration sample identifier and sampling unit;
- target risk \(\epsilon\);
- confidence error \(\delta\);
- Clopper–Pearson bounds and selected threshold;
- date and validity window.

The runtime uses the certified threshold only for matching model/policy/tool/outcome versions.

## 3. Runtime decision order

1. Formal UCON evaluates current admission conditions and obligations.
2. A formal denial immediately denies the workflow.
3. The predictive score is computed for the candidate workflow/plan.
4. The score is compared with the certified threshold.
5. If no threshold was certified, predictive admission abstains/escalates; formal UCON remains active.
6. If the score is outside the accepted set, the controller may restrict, request human approval, or deny according to the risk policy.
7. Every actual tool operation still passes through the formal execution gate and version/revocation checks.
8. At workflow completion, the outcome is recorded using the declared reference semantics.

The predictive threshold does not replace per-action authorization, obligations, mutation verification, or transaction controls.

## 4. Guarantee interpretation

The statistical guarantee is marginal over accepted workflow episodes in the calibrated target population:

\[
\Pr(Y=1\mid Q\le\hat t)\le\epsilon
\]

with calibration confidence at least \(1-\delta\), under the assumptions in G5.3.

It is not a pointwise guarantee for a specific workflow context. It does not guarantee zero unsafe episodes. The formal enforcement layer remains mandatory.

## 5. Dynamic and open-ended workflows

If an agent changes its action plan after admission, the fixed-plan score no longer describes the realized plan. The system must either:

- treat the complete proposed plan as immutable for the certified episode;
- recompute risk for the remaining suffix using a separately calibrated admission unit and valid sampling design; or
- calibrate a policy-induced trajectory distribution that includes the agent's action-selection mechanism.

Repeatedly applying one threshold to highly overlapping workflow prefixes does not create independent calibration observations.

## 6. Version invalidation

A change in any of the following invalidates the certificate unless a transport/compatibility argument is established:

- JEPA weights or architecture;
- action/context encoder;
- risk-score function;
- rollout horizon or candidate-plan generator;
- policy semantics or policy version;
- tool schema/implementation;
- environment distribution;
- outcome definition or evaluator.

A new policy version may be separately certified. Historical replay remains a formal policy evaluation task and is not a substitute for predictive recalibration.

## 7. Production label censoring

When UCON blocks a harmful operation, the actual prohibited effect is not observed. The calibration label must therefore be obtained from controlled sandbox execution, digital-twin simulation, safe replay, or a rigorously defined intervention/near-miss outcome.

If sandbox labels are used, the finite-sample theorem controls the sandbox/reference distribution. A production claim requires separate evidence that the relevant transition and outcome distributions transfer. Do not silently equate simulator risk with production risk.

## 8. Fail-safe behaviour

- Formal denial: deny, irrespective of JEPA.
- Missing or invalid certificate: predictive admission abstains/escalates.
- Version mismatch: invalidate certificate.
- High uncertainty/OOD: abstain or escalate; do not infer malicious intent.
- Missing outcome evidence: mark episode censored/unknown, not safe.
- Failed tool verification: contain/reconcile; do not record successful completion.
