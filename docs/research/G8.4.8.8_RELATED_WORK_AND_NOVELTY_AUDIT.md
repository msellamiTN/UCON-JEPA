# G8.4.8.8 — Related-work and novelty audit

**Audit cut-off:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** targeted prior-art audit; not an exhaustive systematic review  
**Applies to:** the capability-overlay intervention design in the G8.4.8.8 causal utility protocol.

## 1. Executive finding

The broad claim that UCON-JEPA is the first predictive runtime safety monitor for agents is not defensible.

- **ProbGuard (ASE 2026)** predicts future LLM-agent safety violations from execution traces using a learned discrete-time Markov chain and triggers threshold-based interventions, including re-prompting and halting. It is direct prior art for proactive probabilistic monitoring and intervention. Its reported results include a 65.37% reduction in unsafe behaviour with 80.4% task completion under re-prompting, and a 93.60% reduction under halting with a larger completion cost.
- **AgentSpec (ICSE 2026)** specifies and enforces runtime constraints on LLM agents through declarative triggers, predicates, and enforcement actions.
- **Runtime Policy Enforcement for MCP-Based LLM Agents (Electronics 2026)** places a deterministic policy enforcement point at the tool-call boundary and combines policy rules, cross-step information-flow labels, and tamper-evident audit.
- **The Verifier Tax (2026)** demonstrates that stronger interception does not automatically imply safe task completion; it motivates measuring completion and recovery alongside blocked or DENY-labelled actions.
- Prescriptive process-monitoring research already studies prediction-to-intervention policies and their outcome effects.
- I-JEPA and V-JEPA establish latent feature prediction; recent action-conditioned JEPA preprints extend this toward planning and multi-agent world models. JEPA usage alone is not a novelty claim.

The defensible question is narrower:

> Does a JEPA-family, policy-shift-aware risk forecast improve the timing of a temporary, task-preserving least-privilege capability overlay, when UCON remains the sole authorization authority, compared with reactive and supervised-triggered overlays?

This is a **candidate contribution**, not an established novelty result. It requires a DTMC/ProbGuard-style comparator, external validation, and the frozen randomized experiment.

## 3. Candidate differentiators — not yet proof of novelty

1. **Authority separation:** predictive risk scores select only whether to activate a restrictive overlay. They cannot grant, deny, or override authorization. Every submitted operation remains subject to the current UCON gate.
2. **Task-preserving restriction:** each task has a versioned intent, completion condition, essential capability set, and nonessential high-risk restricted set. A finite allowed-action witness demonstrates that the declared task can be completed without any restricted capability under the stated feasible-context preconditions.
3. **Temporary capability overlay:** predictive or reactive triggers activate a one-shot, two-decision capability restriction; the overlay can only remove capabilities and restores the original set after expiry.
4. **Policy-shift evaluation:** the intervention is tested under explicit changes in agent-policy parameters, including shifted and reverse-shift regimes.
5. **Strategy-level randomized evaluation:** episode assignment compares UCON-only, reactive overlay, JEPA-triggered overlay, and supervised-triggered overlay. The estimand is the intention-to-treat effect of assignment to the complete adaptive strategy, not an effect among triggered episodes.
6. **Task-completion guardrail:** the experiment includes explicit task-completion non-inferiority against each comparator, rather than treating more blocking as automatically better.

These distinctions are architecture- and study-specific. The conditional ITT identification proposition is a standard consequence of blocked randomization assumptions, not a new general causal theorem.

## 4. Claims that must be prohibited or qualified

1. **“First predictive runtime monitor for LLM agents.”** Refuted by ProbGuard.
2. **“First runtime enforcement for LLM/MCP agents.”** Refuted by AgentSpec and MCP runtime enforcement.
3. **“First action-conditioned JEPA world model.”** Refuted by action-conditioned JEPA planning and multi-agent world-model preprints.
4. **“Causal/counterfactual risk prediction.”** Not established by observational trajectory prediction or action conditioning alone.
5. **“The overlay provides authorization.”** False. UCON is the sole authorization authority; the overlay is an additional restriction.
6. **“Reduced DENY burden means fewer breaches or less realized harm.”** False. The primary endpoint is DENY-labelled proposals under the synthetic policy oracle, not successful compromise or real-world loss.
7. **“Validated on real LLM agents.”** False until a real tool-using agent loop or benchmark is evaluated.
8. **“JEPA is superior to supervised prediction.”** Not established by prior G8.4 results; no superiority is presumed.
9. **“The capability overlay is a new access-control mechanism.”** Not defensible by itself; least privilege and runtime policy enforcement are established ideas.

Preferred positioning:

> We evaluate a JEPA-triggered, task-preserving capability-overlay strategy composed with an independently authoritative UCON enforcement layer, under policy shift and episode-level randomized assignment.

## 5. Causal and measurement boundaries

The blocked episode randomization identifies the strategy-assignment ITT contrast only if consistency, within-block randomization, positivity, no interference, complete outcome ascertainment, and non-differential censoring hold.

It does not identify:
- the effect among episodes that actually triggered an overlay;
- the effect of an overlay in isolation while holding all later actions fixed;
- an action-level counterfactual outcome;
- external validity to real LLM agents;
- a reduction in successful security breaches or realized harm.

The primary endpoint is the number of submitted proposals whose contemporaneous UCON oracle verdict is DENY. It is a proposal-burden proxy. Report overlay suppression, task completion, safe task completion, post-DENY recovery, and intervention frequency separately. Do not relabel the primary endpoint as a breach rate.

The simulator's capability-aware replan is deliberately synthetic: if a base-policy candidate is unavailable under the overlay, the policy selects uniformly from the currently available set. This uses no oracle verdict, predictive score, future label, or future state. It is not a deployed LLM's replanning behaviour.

## 6. Required comparison and validation work

### Required before a comparative novelty or superiority claim

- **Implementation status:** a first-order context-state DTMC with absorbing DENY/SAFE_END states, validation-only episode-max thresholds, held-out prediction-only evaluation, and a DTMC-triggered pilot overlay arm are now implemented. This is a transparent DTMC-style comparator, **not** a faithful ProbGuard reproduction. The held-out metrics and pilot artifacts must still be executed and independently reviewed before making comparative claims.
- Retain the supervised predictor and reactive-overlay comparator. The four-arm confirmatory ITT design remains unchanged; the DTMC overlay is pilot-only and is not a fifth confirmatory arm.
- Run the predeclared random-trigger overlay as a separate negative-control pilot; do not add it retrospectively to the four-arm confirmatory trial.
- Report JEPA and supervised parameter counts, training budgets, and calibration/threshold support.
- Publish complete arm × seed × archetype × family × task-difficulty tables and per-episode records.

### Required for external validity

A later study must use a real tool-using agent loop or a recognized agent benchmark. Compare UCON-only, reactive overlay, JEPA-triggered overlay, supervised/DTMC-triggered overlay, and a well-defined agent replanning policy. Measure safe task completion, unsafe proposals, actual UCON DENYs, overlay suppressions, recovery after DENY, completion latency, token/tool cost, and intervention burden.

### Required for action-level causal claims

If a future paper claims action-specific counterfactual risk or the causal effect of an individual tool action, introduce randomized action interventions or defensible identification assumptions, explicit overlap/positivity, and validation against observed interventional outcomes. Episode-level strategy randomization does not identify those quantities.

## 7. Positioning recommendation

The paper should not claim that JEPA replaces access control or that it is a better ProbGuard. Its candidate contribution is the composition of:

1. a JEPA-family predictive trigger under policy shift;
2. a temporary, task-preserving capability restriction with explicit essential-capability invariants;
3. a UCON-only authorization boundary that is never bypassed;
4. a strategy-level randomized evaluation with reactive and supervised controls and task-completion non-inferiority.

The predictive model contribution is empirical, the overlay/authorization separation is architectural and assurance-oriented, and the causal result is simulator-bounded. These must remain separate claims.
