# G8.4.8.8 — Related-work and novelty audit

**Audit cut-off:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** targeted prior-art audit; not an exhaustive systematic review  
**Purpose:** constrain the scientific claims before confirmatory execution and manuscript drafting.

## 1. Executive finding

The broad claim that UCON-JEPA is the first predictive runtime safety monitor for autonomous or LLM agents is not defensible.

Three strands of prior work are especially close:

1. **ProbGuard (ASE 2026)** predicts future agent-safety violations from execution traces using a learned discrete-time Markov chain and triggers threshold-based interventions, including re-prompting and halting. It is direct prior art for proactive probabilistic monitoring and intervention.
2. **AgentSpec (ICSE 2026)** specifies and enforces runtime constraints on LLM agents through declarative triggers, predicates, and enforcement actions. It is prior art for configurable runtime enforcement.
3. **Runtime Policy Enforcement for MCP-Based LLM Agents (Electronics 2026)** places a deterministic policy enforcement point at the tool-call boundary and combines rules, cross-step information-flow labels, and tamper-evident audit. It is close prior art for the enforcement plane.

The intervention-utility claim is also adjacent to **The Verifier Tax (2026)** and to prescriptive process-monitoring research. ProbGuard reports a 65.37% reduction in unsafe behaviour with 80.4% baseline task completion under re-prompting, and a 93.60% reduction under halting with a larger completion cost. The Verifier Tax reports that interception does not automatically translate into safe task completion: safe success remains below 5% in most evaluated settings, with post-block recovery often low. These results make safe completion and recovery mandatory secondary outcomes in our own evaluation. The use of JEPA is not by itself a novelty claim: I-JEPA and V-JEPA establish latent feature prediction, while recent preprints such as ActWorld-JEPA and MA-JEPA extend JEPA-style prediction toward action-conditioned planning and multi-agent world models.

The defensible research question is therefore narrower:

> Can a JEPA-family latent predictive model provide useful, policy-shift-aware risk advisories in a system where authorization remains exclusively controlled by a formally specified UCON enforcement layer, and can the advisory's incremental utility be isolated without weakening the enforcement guarantee?

This is a **candidate contribution**, not yet an established novelty result. It must be supported by comparative experiments and explicit non-interference evidence.

### 2.1 Key distinctions that can still matter

- **Authority separation:** the predictor is not an authorization component. It cannot grant, deny, override, or bypass a UCON decision. The enforcement plane is the only component allowed to produce an authorization outcome.
- **Policy-shift focus:** the predictive model is evaluated under explicit changes in agent-policy parameters, including shifted and reverse-shift conditions, rather than only under a fixed in-distribution trace generator.
- **Predictive representation family:** the risk model is a JEPA-family latent predictor, not a DTMC over symbolic states. This is an architectural distinction, not evidence of superiority.
- **Intervention assignment:** the proposed four-arm design isolates advisory warnings from reactive UCON, supervised risk warnings, and random warnings. The primary estimand is the episode-level intention-to-treat effect of assigning the advisory policy under a specified synthetic response mechanism.
- **Non-interference:** the project can contribute a precise separation contract and executable invariance evidence showing that predictive scores and warnings never alter the formal authorization result for a fixed state/action pair.

None of these distinctions alone proves novelty. The manuscript must compare them experimentally and formally against the closest prior systems.

## 3. Claims that must be prohibited or qualified

The following claims must not appear without new evidence:

1. **“First predictive runtime monitor for LLM agents.”** Refuted by ProbGuard.
2. **“First JEPA-based action-conditioned world model.”** Refuted by the action-conditioned JEPA planning literature, including ActWorld-JEPA and MA-JEPA.
3. **“Causal or counterfactual risk prediction.”** Not established by observational trajectory prediction or action conditioning alone.
4. **“JEPA is superior to supervised prediction.”** Not supported by the current temporal-support results; those results are mixed and show no consistent JEPA predictive advantage.
5. **“Validated on real LLM agents.”** The G8.4.8.8 simulator uses a synthetic action-substitution response model, not a deployed or benchmarked LLM agent.
6. **“The warning improves task safety in general.”** Any effect is conditional on the synthetic environment, response propensity, action vocabulary, policy archetypes, and tested resource families.
7. **“Formal safety is provided by JEPA.”** The only safety authority is the UCON oracle and online enforcement gate. Prediction is advisory.

Preferred language: *“We investigate a JEPA-family predictive advisory layer under an independently authoritative UCON enforcement architecture.”*

## 4. Critical causal and identification boundary

The current model is trained on observed state/action trajectories and performs recursive latent prediction. This supports a predictive claim only for distributions represented by the data and tested shifts.

It does **not** automatically identify:
- the effect of forcing a particular alternative action;
- the outcome distribution under an intervention on an action;
- the causal effect of a warning outside the simulator's specified response mechanism;
- an intervention-sufficient or identifiable latent state.

The G8.4.8.8 randomized assignment can identify the intention-to-treat effect of assigning a warning arm **inside the synthetic simulator**, because the arm assignment and response coins are randomized by design. It does not establish causal validity of the JEPA's action-conditioned rollouts or external validity to real agents.

The current response model chooses an alternative action uniformly from the same family vocabulary with probability q. It is intentionally transparent, but it is not a learned replanner, an LLM re-prompting policy, or a risk-aware alternative-action selector. The paper must say so.

## 5. Required comparison and validation work

### Required before a strong novelty claim

- Add a **DTMC / ProbGuard-style baseline** over the same symbolic state traces, with the same calibration partition, horizon, warning budget, intervention response model, and episode randomization. Report it as a comparator, not as a reproduction of ProbGuard unless its full method is faithfully implemented.
- Keep the existing supervised predictor and random-warning arms.
- Compare calibration and shift robustness separately from intervention utility. A predictor can have useful intervention utility without having the best AUROC, but that must be demonstrated by the randomized experiment.
- Report the JEPA and supervised parameter counts and training budgets. Do not describe the current comparator as strictly capacity-matched unless the parameterization is corrected.
- Report the exact synthetic policy and response assumptions, including q, rho, archetypes, action vocabulary, and episode horizon.
- Retain all per-episode outcomes, assignments, calibration thresholds, model checkpoints, runtime versions, and SHA-256 manifests.

### Required for external validity

A later study must use a real agent loop or a recognized tool-use benchmark (for example, an MCP tool-call harness or an established agent benchmark). It should compare:
- reactive UCON only;
- predictive JEPA advisory;
- a strong supervised or DTMC predictor;
- a random-warning control;
- a real replanning/re-prompting response policy.

That study should measure safe task completion, unsafe action attempts, DENY burden, recovery after DENY, completion latency, token/tool cost, and warning burden. The synthetic G8.4.8.8 results cannot substitute for it.

### Required for interventional prediction claims

If a future paper claims action-specific counterfactual risk, it must introduce an identification design with randomized action interventions or defensible causal assumptions, explicit overlap/positivity, and validation against observed interventional outcomes. The current warning-arm randomization alone does not identify those action-level effects.

## 6. Positioning recommendation

The strongest defensible paper is not “JEPA replaces runtime access control” and not “JEPA is a better ProbGuard.” It is:

> **A predictive advisory architecture for policy-shifted agent trajectories, composed with a formally authoritative UCON enforcement layer, with explicit authority non-interference and a controlled evaluation of advisory utility.**

The contributions should be separated into:

1. **Architecture:** predictive advisory plane / authoritative UCON enforcement plane, with an explicit non-interference contract.
2. **Predictive evaluation:** recursive JEPA-family risk forecasting under policy shift, compared with supervised and DTMC baselines.
3. **Intervention evaluation:** episode-randomized warning utility under transparent response propensities, with random-warning and reactive-UCON controls.
4. **Assurance:** executable tests and formal arguments for the authorization boundary; no claim that predictor uncertainty itself is a safety guarantee.

The first contribution is primarily architectural/formal, the second empirical, the third causal but simulator-bounded, and the fourth assurance-oriented. They must not be collapsed into one unsupported “safe JEPA” claim.
