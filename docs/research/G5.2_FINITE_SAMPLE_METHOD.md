# G5.2 — Finite-sample workflow trajectory risk certification

**Status:** Method selected as a principled adaptation of an existing selective-risk procedure; not claimed as a new generic statistical method.

## 1. Method selection

We select a one-sided exact Clopper–Pearson upper confidence bound combined with fixed-sequence threshold testing.

The method suits binary episode-level trajectory outcomes, a finite pre-specified ordered threshold family, a marginal risk target among accepted episodes, and data-dependent threshold selection.

It is closely related to Dong, Hao, and Shinnou (2026), *Fixed-Sequence Confidence-Interval Calibration for Finite-Sample Selective Risk Control*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/stats9050109. That work establishes a generic fixed-sequence selective-risk procedure for IID observations. UCON-JEPA must not claim that generic statistical construction as original. Its research task is to instantiate and validate it for complete workflow trajectories, security-specific bad events, policy/tool versioning, and the formal/predictive enforcement split.

## 2. Frozen candidate family

Let:

\[
\mathcal T=\{t_1<t_2<\cdots<t_K\}
\]

be fixed independently of the calibration sample. It may be pre-registered or selected using a disjoint pilot sample.

For each threshold:

\[
S_{i,k}=\mathbf 1\{Q_i\le t_k\},
\quad
n_k=\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{i,k},
\quad
m_k=\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{i,k}Y_i.
\]

The empirical accepted-episode risk is \(m_k/n_k\) when \(n_k>0\).

## 3. Exact one-sided upper bound

For confidence error \(\delta\in(0,1)\), define:

\[
U_k=
\begin{cases}
1,&n_k=0,\\
1,&m_k=n_k,\\
F^{-1}_{\mathrm{Beta}(m_k+1,n_k-m_k)}(1-\delta),
&0\le m_k<n_k.
\end{cases}
\]

This is the one-sided Clopper–Pearson upper confidence bound for accepted-episode Bernoulli risk at fixed threshold \(t_k\).

A threshold is certified if \(U_k\le\epsilon\).

## 4. Fixed-sequence rule

Inspect thresholds in ascending order. Continue while the current threshold is certified; stop at the first failure. Return the last certified threshold, or NULL if the first candidate fails.

The returned threshold is the most permissive threshold in the certified prefix. The procedure may fail to reach a later safe region if population risk is non-monotone. This is a power/coverage trade-off, not a validity failure.

## 5. Why not choose the best threshold after inspecting all calibration outcomes?

Selecting the largest empirical-coverage threshold that happens to pass an uncorrected empirical risk test is a data-dependent multiple-selection procedure. Its empirical risk is optimistically biased. Fixed-sequence testing provides a finite-sample guarantee for the returned threshold under the stated IID and pre-specification assumptions.

## 6. Alternative

A simultaneous Bonferroni procedure computes each bound at confidence error \(\delta/K\) and may select any certified threshold. It is more conservative but can reach later safe regions after an intermediate unsafe threshold. It is retained as an experimental baseline and possible deployment alternative.

## 7. Scope and non-claims

- No monotonicity of \(R(t)\) is required for fixed-sequence validity.
- The threshold family must be independent of the calibration sample.
- The model and score construction must be frozen.
- The risk guarantee is marginal among accepted workflow episodes.
- The IID sampling assumption is substantive.
- This is not a pointwise conditional-risk guarantee.
- This is not a production guarantee when labels come only from a mismatched simulator.
- The procedure does not replace UCON authorization or the runtime safety monitor.
