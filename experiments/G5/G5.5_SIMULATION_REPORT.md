# G5.5 — Synthetic simulation report

**Run ID:** G5-IID-SYNTH-2026-11-18-01  
**Status:** Preliminary synthetic validation; not a JEPA or production experiment.

## Configuration

- Random seed: 20261118
- Monte Carlo replications: 2,000 per scenario
- Calibration episodes per replication: 1,500
- Target selective risk: epsilon = 0.10
- Confidence error: delta = 0.05
- Score: Uniform(0,1)
- Candidate thresholds: 19 fixed equally spaced values from 0.10 to 1.00
- Outcome: one binary bad-trajectory indicator per complete IID episode
- Population risk: numerically integrated from the known data-generating process
- Methods: fixed-sequence CP, simultaneous Bonferroni CP, naive empirical selection

The script is in trajectory_risk_control.py. Raw aggregate output is in results/simulation_results.csv.

| Infeasible | Naive empirical | 0.0990 | 0.01475 | 0.9010 | 0.11068 |

The Monte Carlo upper bound is a one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper confidence bound on the unsafe-return frequency over the 2,000 replications. It quantifies Monte Carlo uncertainty; it is not the same as the per-calibration statistical guarantee.

## Findings

1. In the monotone scenario, fixed-sequence CP stayed below the nominal delta = 0.05 failure probability and retained more mean acceptance than Bonferroni CP.
2. In the non-monotone scenario, fixed-sequence testing stopped at an early uncertified candidate and almost always returned NULL. Bonferroni could certify a later safe region, at the cost of stricter simultaneous bounds.
3. In the infeasible scenario, both certified methods almost always returned NULL. This is correct fail-safe behaviour; the naive method sometimes returned a threshold despite the population risk being above target.
4. Naive empirical selection substantially exceeded the nominal unsafe-return probability in all three scenarios.

## Limitations

- This validates only the statistical procedure under the synthetic IID episode model.
- It does not train or evaluate JEPA.
- It does not validate real workflow independence.
- It does not establish production transfer from sandbox labels.
- It does not model adaptive agent actions, hidden side effects, or delayed labels.
- The synthetic risk curves and sample sizes are illustrative, not operational recommendations.

## Next experiments

The next experimental extension should replace IID synthetic episodes with clustered tenant/session episodes and compare cluster-level calibration, episode subsampling, and dependence-aware methods. It should then use labelled sandbox workflow traces from the four target domains. No deployment threshold should be selected from this synthetic run.
