## 1. Implemented predictors and information boundary

Four next-action predictors are compared:

1. **Majority:** family-conditioned action frequencies fitted on the training partition.
2. **Historical frequency:** within-episode action-history counts from the current context, smoothed by a training-only family prior.
3. **First-order Markov:** (P(a_t \mid a_{t-1}, family)), fitted from training episodes. At inference it receives the immediately preceding observed action only; the first transition uses a BOS prior.
4. **Past-only softmax:** regularized multinomial linear predictor over the 34-dimensional decision-time context.

The supervised predictor API accepts context only. The Markov API additionally accepts the previous observed action. Neither accepts future actions, future observations, security labels, policy identity, episode ID, seed, oracle reason or partition. Family is encoded in the context. Policy/family metadata is used only to stratify evaluation output.

Train-only feature standardization and model fitting are used. Validation selects the training checkpoint. A scalar temperature is selected on the separate calibration partition. Test is held out from model selection and calibration.

The Markov temporal-leakage test mutates the realized action at time (t), recomputes past-action inputs, and verifies that the prediction at (t) is unchanged while the changed action becomes available only to the next transition (t+1).

## 2. In-policy held-out episode test

Single seed: `20261120`; 300 episodes per policy/family pair; five behavior policies; three families; eight transitions per episode. The episode-disjoint test partition contains 7,080 transitions.

| Predictor | NLL ↓ | Top-1 ↑ | Top-2 ↑ | Multiclass Brier ↓ | Top-label ECE ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Majority | 1.0478 | 45.07% | 80.07% | 0.6344 | 0.0013 |
| Historical frequency | 0.9432 | 50.66% | 75.14% | 0.5509 | 0.1454 |
| First-order Markov | 0.9268 | 56.40% | **86.07%** | 0.5587 | 0.0233 |
| Past-only softmax | **0.7970** | **63.26%** | 84.46% | **0.4586** | 0.0301 |

The softmax model improves NLL, top-1 accuracy and Brier score over these baselines on this in-policy test. The Markov baseline retains the best top-2 accuracy. No single predictor wins every metric.

### Calibration check

The calibration partition selected temperature (T=1.05). On test, calibrated softmax metrics were:
- NLL: 0.7973 (raw 0.7970);
- Brier: 0.4594 (raw 0.4586);
- top-label ECE: 0.0337 (raw 0.0301).

This temperature adjustment did **not** improve these test metrics. Calibration cannot be described as successful on this run.

The softmax model does not lead on LOPO macro NLL, top-1, top-2 or Brier. Its fold performance is highly heterogeneous: it helps on the two synthetic workflow-style policies, but fails badly on the uniform and retrying-adversary held-out policies. Historical frequency is particularly strong on retrying-adversary because the policy repeats its previously denied action, a pattern directly exposed in past action history.

These results do not establish generalization to unseen real agent policies. They demonstrate that behavior-policy shift materially changes action forecast quality and that a simple history baseline can outperform the learned model.

## 4. Scientific interpretation

- **In-policy next-action prediction:** encouraging but preliminary. The supervised model improves three of five reported metrics against the declared baselines on one synthetic seed; Markov is better on top-2.
- **LOPO policy generalization:** not supported by this run. The learned predictor loses to the historical-frequency baseline on macro NLL, top-1, top-2 and Brier, with severe failures on two held-out policy types.
- **Calibration:** the fitted temperature does not improve held-out test calibration or proper scoring metrics.
- **JEPA uncertain-action forecasting:** not tested in this increment.
- **Joint action/state JEPA:** not tested.
- **Operational intervention value:** not tested.
- **Real-agent generalization:** not tested.

No confidence intervals, multi-seed variability estimates, or significance claims are available. The synthetic policies are hand-designed stress regimes; the test episodes are not a sample of production agent populations.

## 5. Decision and next gate

**G8.4.8.3 implementation, leakage tests, single-seed execution and CI validation passed.** The scientific result is mixed and specifically cautions against assuming action-policy generalization.

Before using the action predictor as the front end of E2, the next increment must:
1. repeat action forecasting across predeclared independent seeds;
2. investigate policy-specific failures and calibration without test-driven tuning;
3. retain matched per-policy/family metrics and episode-level uncertainty;
4. freeze the action-model selection rule before E2;
5. keep formal UCON as the sole current-action authorization authority.

The predictor remains advisory. It cannot authorize, execute or override a formal UCON DENY.
