## Executed protocol

- Seeds: 2041, 2042, 2043; 1,200 generated episodes per policy family and seed.
- Horizons: 1, 2, 4 and 8 transitions.
- Episode-disjoint train/validation/test partitions inherited from G8.4.3.
- Windows remain within one episode and require contiguous step indices.
- Horizon violation target: any denial in the next \(h\) transitions.
- Terminal targets: \((approved,sensitive\_read,role\_granted)\) after transition \(h\).
- **Direct JEPA:** horizon-specific latent predictor with EMA target encoder.
- **Capacity-matched direct-supervision control:** same linear context/action encoders, predictor, decoder dimensions and parameter count as JEPA; trained on security labels only, without EMA target or latent-prediction objective.
- **Ridge reference:** same context and ordered action-sequence inputs and security targets.
- **Recursive JEPA and recursive supervised control:** one-step models rolled forward in latent space using the supplied ordered action sequence.
- Fixed risk threshold: 0.5. No threshold was tuned on test data.
- Paired, seed-stratified episode-cluster bootstrap with 2,000 replicates; whole episodes resampled within each seed and equal seed weighting.

## Interpretation

1. **Direct prediction:** the ridge reference is strongest across all four horizons. The capacity-matched supervised control also outperforms direct JEPA on Brier and security MSE at every horizon; the paired intervals exclude zero in the direction favouring direct supervision.
2. **Recursive latent rollout:** a more nuanced result appears. Recursive JEPA has materially lower Brier and security MSE than the capacity-matched recursively rolled supervised control at horizons 2, 4 and 8. The seed-stratified paired intervals exclude zero. This is evidence that the JEPA latent-prediction objective may provide a more rollout-compatible representation than direct security supervision in this specific linear model.
3. **This relative recursive advantage is not an absolute success.** At horizon 8, recursive JEPA AUROC is 0.3926, AUPRC 0.0602, Brier 0.3342 and FPR 0.5356. It is better than the even more unstable recursively rolled supervised control, but still unsuitable as a risk predictor. The direct ridge baseline remains substantially better.
4. The recursively rolled supervised control is intentionally a diagnostic comparator: direct security supervision does not guarantee that its latent state is suitable for autoregressive rollout. Its failure is informative about the objective/representation difference, but does not by itself establish a general JEPA advantage.
5. Long-horizon populations are conditional on episodes surviving until the forecast origin and containing a complete window. Episode termination occurs at the first denied action, so horizons do not share identical test populations.
6. The supplied future action sequence is known to both rollout models. This is not a model of an adaptive agent's future action policy.
7. The models and policy environment are synthetic linear references. No real-world security, causal, deployment or formal-assurance claim follows.

## Research decision

**Preserve a qualified finding, not a blanket JEPA superiority claim.**

- Direct horizon prediction: no JEPA advantage; supervised controls and ridge are stronger.
- Recursive latent rollout: JEPA's latent objective outperforms the capacity-matched direct-supervision control on the tested recursive error metrics at horizons 2, 4 and 8.
- Operational quality: even the recursive JEPA is poor at horizon 8 and loses to direct ridge. No proactive security utility has been demonstrated.

Next, investigate why the JEPA latent objective is more rollout-compatible than direct supervision while remaining weak in absolute terms. Priorities: improve the state decoder and action-sequence encoder, add a stronger temporal supervised baseline, evaluate on non-censored fixed-length trajectories, calibrate on a separate partition, and measure lead time against the reactive formal UCON oracle. Formal UCON remains the sole authorization authority.
