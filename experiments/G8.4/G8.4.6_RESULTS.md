## Executed protocol

- Seeds: 2041, 2042, 2043.
- 1,200 generated episodes per policy family and seed.
- Horizons: 1, 2, 4 and 8 transitions.
- Episode-disjoint train/validation/test partitions inherited from G8.4.3.
- Only contiguous windows wholly inside one episode are admitted.
- Horizon target: any denial in the next \(h\) transitions and terminal workflow flags after transition \(h\).
- Direct JEPA: horizon-specific predictor with EMA target encoder.
- Recursive JEPA: one-step predictor rolled forward in latent space; cumulative risk \(1-\prod_j(1-\hat p_j)\).
- Supervised comparator: ridge model with the same initial context and ordered action sequence, directly predicting the same four security targets.
- Fixed risk threshold: 0.5. No threshold selection on test data.
- 2,000 paired episode-cluster bootstrap replicates for candidate-minus-supervised Brier and security-MSE differences.

The recursive rollout uses the one-step model's own train-fitted preprocessing parameters. It receives the future action sequence as an input; it does not predict the agent's future policy.

## Findings

1. **The supervised comparator is better at every evaluated horizon** on AUROC, AUPRC and both error metrics. The paired episode bootstrap intervals for Brier and security MSE remain positive for all JEPA comparisons.
2. **Recursive latent rollout degrades sharply with horizon.** At h=8, AUROC is 0.3926, AUPRC 0.0602, Brier 0.3342 and false-positive rate 0.5356. This is consistent with compounding latent transition and decoder errors.
3. Direct JEPA's h=8 Brier is lower than its h=1 Brier, but this is not evidence that its forecasting improved: the target prevalence, test windows and conditional sample population change with horizon. Its AUROC falls to 0.5833 and recall at 0.5 is zero.
4. The supervised h=8 model also has zero recall at the fixed 0.5 threshold despite AUROC 0.9682. This points to an operating-threshold/calibration issue under rare long-horizon labels; the ranking metric alone does not establish an operational intervention policy.
5. Longer windows are conditional on an episode surviving until the horizon. Episodes stop at the first denied action, so horizons are not evaluated on identical populations.
6. The experiment uses synthetic policy semantics and a linear reference JEPA. It does not establish real-world risk anticipation, causal validity, security guarantees, or deployment readiness.

## Research decision

**Do not claim a JEPA multi-step advantage.** The current model is a transparent linear reference implementation and is not competitive with the matched supervised baseline on this benchmark. Recursive rollout is especially fragile.

The next scientific increment should not simply increase model size. First address the identifiable methodological and modelling questions:

- Make horizon-specific test populations comparable, or explicitly model censoring/termination.
- Add a learned action-sequence encoder and a stronger temporal supervised baseline with matched capacity and tuning budget.
- Diagnose latent variance/collapse and target/predictor alignment by horizon.
- Separate risk ranking, probability calibration and decision-threshold selection using a dedicated calibration partition.
- Measure predictive lead time against the formal reactive UCON baseline while preserving the invariant that JEPA cannot override DENY.

Formal UCON remains the only authorization authority.
