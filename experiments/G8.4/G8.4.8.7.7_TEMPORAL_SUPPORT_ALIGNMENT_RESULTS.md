### Calibration-direction anomaly

The JEPA E2 C0 calibration slope was negative for exactly one of five seeds:

- Seed 20261122: C0 slope `-0.1750`; CA slope `+1.1374`.
- On that seed's pooled shifted H=1 test windows, raw AUROC was 0.7394, C0 AUROC fell to 0.2606, and CA AUROC remained 0.7394.
- The other four JEPA C0 slopes were positive (0.0835–0.1967). All CA slopes were positive (1.1374–1.4452).
- CP also exposes position-specific calibration heterogeneity: for seed 20261122, the position-0 CP slope is the same negative fit as C0, while positions 1–7 have positive slopes.

The predeclared paired whole-episode bootstrap for CA-minus-C0 Brier identifies the same seed-specific anomaly: for JEPA seed 20261122 the difference is `-0.233492`, with 95% percentile interval `[-0.241631, -0.225175]`. The other four seed-level point estimates are positive (+0.001319 to +0.007175), so the direction is not consistent across seeds. These are conditional within-seed intervals, not a five-seed population interval.

At H=8 there is only start position 0; C0, CP and CA are identical by construction. This is a useful negative control for the temporal-support manipulation.

## Scientific interpretation

1. The severe pooled H=1 AUROC reversal is reproducible for one JEPA seed, not a stable five-seed phenomenon.
2. The anomaly is associated with an unstable negative-slope position-0 calibrator being applied to the pooled temporal-position mixture. The all-position CA fit avoids that particular inversion in the affected seed.
3. The data support a **seed-sensitive calibration/support interaction**, not a general claim that temporal-support mismatch explains all calibration instability. The four other JEPA seeds do not show the same inversion, and CA does not uniformly reduce Brier versus C0 across seeds.
4. CP and CA produce broadly similar five-seed H=1 Brier/ECE means. Position-level results must remain visible; pooled metrics alone hide the seed-20261122 failure.
5. The supervised comparator has better mean H=1 raw AUROC and lower calibrated Brier than JEPA. No JEPA predictive advantage is established.
6. The model-capacity report is 852 trainable JEPA parameters versus 776 supervised parameters (ratio 0.911). Therefore the comparison is not exactly parameter-matched; do not describe it as a strict capacity-matched comparison without qualification.
7. These results are limited to the declared synthetic benchmark. They do not establish real-agent transfer, intervention utility, operational calibration or production security assurance. Calibration remains offline and cannot influence UCON authorization.
