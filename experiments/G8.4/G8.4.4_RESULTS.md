## Protocol actually executed

- Seeds: 2026, 2027, 2028.
- 200 generated episodes per policy family per seed; IAM, data export, SoD.
- Episode-disjoint hash split; feature standardization parameters fit on training rows only.
- 100 JEPA training epochs, latent dimension 12, fixed configuration in \`predictive_benchmark.py\`.
- Fixed 0.5 test intervention threshold; no test-set threshold optimization.
- Comparators: direct supervised context+action regression, context-only and action-only ablations.
- Test sample sizes: 347, 338, and 335 rows respectively (1,020 total test transitions across seeds); rows are clustered within episodes.
- Metrics are computed on the synthetic policy oracle labels. Across-seed standard deviations are descriptive only; they are not confidence intervals.

## Interpretation

1. **The initial JEPA reference does not beat the matched supervised baseline.** The direct supervised predictor has higher AUROC/AUPRC, lower Brier score, much lower security-feature MSE, and substantially higher recall at the fixed threshold.
2. JEPA has a lower false-positive rate at 0.5, but its recall is only 0.1846 on average. This is a conservative operating point with many missed violations, not evidence of a better risk model.
3. JEPA's ECE-10 is 0.1678, worse than the supervised baseline (0.1431) and context/action ablations. Neither model should be described as calibrated from this single small synthetic experiment.
4. The supervised baseline's ECE is also non-negligible. Calibration requires a separate held-out calibration protocol and uncertainty analysis.
5. The small seed count, synthetic context distribution, row-level metrics with within-episode dependence, simple linear architecture, and shared written policy semantics limit inference.

## Research decision

**Do not claim JEPA superiority.** This result is adverse to the current linear JEPA configuration on the primary predictive metrics. Preserve it as a null/negative result and use it to guide the next controlled increment.

Next experiments must:
- compare episode-clustered uncertainty intervals and paired per-episode differences;
- audit feature/label construction and inspect per-family errors;
- evaluate compositional action-sequence holdouts and distribution shift;
- tune intervention thresholds on a distinct validation/calibration partition, then freeze them before test;
- assess selective risk, false interventions, lead time, and operational utility;
- compare richer sequence encoders and probabilistic predictors under matched budgets.

No result here establishes real-world security efficacy, causal benefit, or production readiness. Formal UCON remains the only authorization authority.
