## 1. Experimental conditions

Three predeclared seeds: `20261120, 20261121, 20261122`. Each dataset has 4,500 episodes and 36,000 transitions. Horizons are (h\in\{1,2,4,8\}). Each E2 window uses 32 autoregressive Monte Carlo action-sequence samples. E1/E2 differences use 1,000 paired whole-episode bootstrap replicates per seed and horizon.

### E1 — privileged known-action diagnostic

E1 receives the realized future action sequence and rolls the JEPA latent predictor forward using those actions. It does not receive future observations. This is a privileged diagnostic, not a deployable forecast.

### E2 — predicted-action rollout

E2 receives the initial context only. A past-only action head predicts a categorical action distribution from the current JEPA latent; E2 samples an action, advances the JEPA latent, and repeats. Future actions, future observations and future security labels are not inputs to the E2 inference function. Monte Carlo variation quantifies sampled action-sequence uncertainty, not model epistemic uncertainty.

The JEPA is the existing transparent linear reference implementation. Its context encoder is trained for latent future-state prediction; the action head is then fitted on the learned context latents. A raw-context action head is included as a one-step diagnostic control only; it is not used in E2 because future raw observations are unavailable during rollout.

The JEPA latent action head is substantially worse than the raw-context action model on NLL, top-1, top-2 and Brier. This indicates that the current JEPA context representation does not preserve enough information for the policy prediction task. It is a representation diagnostic, not proof that all JEPA representations must lose action information.

Goal-directed test episodes have no positive DENY outcomes in several strata, so AUROC is undefined there; Brier remains informative about false-positive risk. The large goal-directed horizon-8 Brier is a warning that the rollout can generate false alarms under this policy regime. These are synthetic policy strata, not real-agent groups.

## 5. Scientific interpretation

1. **E1 is a strong privileged reference.** Supplying realized future actions yields materially better risk prediction than E2. The gap is expected from the information advantage, but its size quantifies the cost of uncertain actions.
2. **Current E2 is not yet adequate for dependable multi-step anticipation.** Risk Brier is worse at every horizon; at horizon 8 AUROC is near chance.
3. **The JEPA latent action head is a bottleneck candidate.** It predicts next actions worse than a raw-context diagnostic. The current latent objective may discard information needed for agent-policy prediction.
4. **Action uncertainty grows with horizon.** Monte Carlo spread increases, but the risk probabilities are not shown to be calibrated under policy shift.
5. **No intervention utility, real-agent generalization, or security assurance claim is established.**

## 6. Decision and next gate

**G8.4.8.4 implementation and three-seed E1/E2 execution passed. The scientific result is negative for the current E2 configuration.**

Do not present E2 as a successful predictive security controller. The next research increment should test a **joint action/state JEPA representation** with an explicit auxiliary action-prediction objective, preserving the EMA target encoder and latent transition predictor while making action-relevant information part of the learned context representation. Compare it against the current JEPA-latent head and raw-context diagnostic using validation-only selection, calibration-only temperature fitting, and the same episode-disjoint multi-horizon test.

Formal UCON remains the only authorization authority. JEPA predictions remain advisory; no predictor may override a formal DENY.
