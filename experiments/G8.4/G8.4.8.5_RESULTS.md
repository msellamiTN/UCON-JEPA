## 1. Model and protocol

Three seeds: `20261120, 20261121, 20261122`; 300 episodes per policy/family pair; eight transitions per episode. Horizons: 1, 2, 4 and 8. E2 uses 32 Monte Carlo action-sequence samples per window. Paired bootstrap intervals use 1,000 whole-episode resamples.

The joint model adds an auxiliary family-masked action cross-entropy head to the existing linear action-conditioned JEPA:

[
L = L_{latent} + \lambda_v L_{variance} + \lambda_s L_{security}
    + \lambda_a L_{action}, \qquad \lambda_a=0.5.
]

The action head predicts the current action from the context latent. Its gradient updates the context encoder and action head jointly. The future target encoder remains EMA-only; the JEPA latent predictor and security decoder objectives remain in place.

Comparators:
- **Post-hoc latent head:** the G8.4.8.4 JEPA with a separately fitted action classifier on its context latents.
- **Raw-context diagnostic:** a separately fitted action predictor on the raw context. It is a one-step diagnostic only and is not used in recursive E2 because future raw context is unavailable.
- **Joint action/state JEPA:** the proposed auxiliary-action-loss model.

E1 continues to use realized future actions and is privileged. E2 receives only the initial context and generates its own autoregressive action samples from the model's action head on predicted JEPA latents. Future actions, future observations and security labels are not E2 inference inputs.

## 2. One-step action prediction

Mean ± sample standard deviation across the three seeds:

| Action head | NLL ↓ | Top-1 ↑ | Top-2 ↑ | Brier ↓ | ECE ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Post-hoc JEPA latent | 0.9204 ± 0.0154 | 55.40% ± 1.09 pp | 81.51% ± 0.89 pp | 0.5456 ± 0.0101 | 0.0341 ± 0.0032 |
| Raw-context diagnostic | **0.7978 ± 0.0004** | **63.13% ± 0.12 pp** | **84.36% ± 0.09 pp** | **0.4598 ± 0.0004** | 0.0333 ± 0.0003 |
| Joint action/state head | 0.8036 ± 0.0032 | 62.74% ± 0.59 pp | 84.22% ± 0.24 pp | 0.4646 ± 0.0028 | 0.0328 ± 0.0051 |

The joint objective recovers most of the action-predictive information lost by the post-hoc JEPA latent: top-1 rises from 55.4% to 62.7%, close to the 63.1% raw-context diagnostic. This supports the representation-bottleneck hypothesis for the tested linear JEPA and synthetic generator. It does not establish that the same auxiliary objective will work for other architectures or real agents.

## 4. Privileged E1 remains an upper reference

For the joint model, E1 known-action violation Brier is 0.1990, 0.2135, 0.1886 and 0.1716 at horizons 1, 2, 4 and 8, respectively. Corresponding E2 Brier is 0.2545, 0.2559, 0.2175 and 0.2413.

E2 remains worse than E1 at every horizon. This is not a surprising failure of the experiment: E1 is given the realized future actions, whereas E2 must forecast them. The difference quantifies the remaining action-uncertainty cost.

## 5. Scientific verdict

- **Representation hypothesis:** supported in this experiment. Joint action loss restores next-action predictability in the JEPA latent relative to the post-hoc latent head.
- **Short-horizon E2:** encouraging. Joint action/state JEPA improves risk Brier, AUROC and security MSE at horizons 1/2/4 in the three-seed means, with paired episode evidence strongest at horizon 1.
- **Long-horizon E2:** not solved. Horizon-8 AUROC remains near chance and paired Brier differences do not support improvement over the post-hoc model.
- **Policy generalization:** open. This experiment is in-policy; G8.4.8.3.1 showed that action-policy shift is a major failure mode.
- **Real-agent generalization, intervention utility, and production security assurance:** not established.

The result supports a specific conclusion: action prediction should be part of representation learning, but this linear joint objective does not yet produce dependable long-horizon risk anticipation.
