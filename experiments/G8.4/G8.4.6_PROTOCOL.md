# G8.4.6 — Multi-step JEPA prediction protocol

## Question

Does action-conditioned latent prediction retain useful predictive information over multiple future transitions, and how does direct horizon prediction compare with recursive one-step latent rollout and a matched supervised predictor?

## Horizons and target definitions

Evaluate horizons \(h \in \{1,2,4,8\}\). A sample starts at state \(x_t\) and supplies the ordered candidate-action sequence \(a_t,\ldots,a_{t+h-1}\).

- **Violation target:** 1 iff at least one policy-oracle denial occurs during the next \(h\) transitions.
- **Terminal-state targets:** \((approved,sensitive\_read,role\_granted)\) after transition \(h\).
- **Direct horizon:** a horizon-specific JEPA predicts the EMA target encoding of the terminal observation and decodes the horizon violation and terminal-state features.
- **Recursive rollout:** a one-step JEPA predicts each next latent in order. The predicted latent is fed into the next prediction step. Cumulative violation risk is \(1-\prod_{j=1}^{h}(1-\hat p_j)\).
- **Supervised comparator:** ridge regression receives the same initial context and flattened ordered action sequence and predicts the same four security targets directly.

The direct and recursive variants answer different questions and are not conflated.

## Splitting and fitting

- Use the existing episode-disjoint train/validation/test split.
- Construct windows only within a single episode and require contiguous step indices.
- Fit context, action-sequence and future-observation scalers on the horizon-specific training windows only.
- Fit all models on training windows only. Validation metrics are diagnostic and do not select thresholds.
- Freeze risk threshold at 0.5 before test evaluation.
- Do not fit on test data or tune from test outcomes.

## Metrics

At each horizon: violation AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, ECE-10, recall and false-positive rate at 0.5; full four-target security MSE; terminal-state MSE. Report window and episode counts. Provide paired episode-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals for candidate-minus-supervised Brier and security-MSE differences. Positive differences favour supervised.

## Interpretation constraints

- Episodes terminate on the first denied action. Longer-horizon windows therefore condition on an episode surviving until the forecast origin and having enough remaining transitions. Report counts and do not treat horizons as identically sampled populations.
- Recursive rollout assumes the future action sequence is supplied. It is not a learned agent-policy forecast.
- The synthetic policy semantics are not an empirical production distribution.
- A good latent loss is not itself evidence of violation anticipation.
- Formal UCON remains the sole authorization authority. Predictive scores cannot authorize a denied action.
